## What breaks, and for whom

A survivor is supposed to be a lead: something the tests do not notice. An **equivalent mutant** is a
survivor that is not a lead at all, because the mutated program behaves identically to the original.
Nothing can kill it, and a reader who chases it wastes the time the tool was meant to save.

LethAL reports both as `survived`, in the same list, with the same fields.

## The measurement

R159's `shift-integer` spike ran seven live sites across four fixtures. Five survived, and one of the
five is provably equivalent:

```
sandbox-hang  HangLogic:33   Counter := 0  ->  Counter := 1     survived
```

`CountUpTo(3)` advances until `Counter >= 3`. From 0 it walks 1, 2, 3; from 1 it walks 2, 3. Both
return 3, on every input, so no test can distinguish them. The other four survivors are ordinary
coverage gaps (values written and never asserted), and acting on those IS worthwhile.

Both appear under `SURVIVORS BY PROCEDURE` with nothing to tell them apart. Evidence:
`docs/superpowers/specs/2026-08-26-r159-shift-integer-spike.md` §OUTCOME, and the run itself.

## Why the existing signals do not cover this

- [[R046]] separates a survivor whose guard never fired from one whose guard did. This mutant's
  guard fired and the mutated line executed: `executionProven` is the RIGHT answer here and says
  nothing about equivalence.
- [[R116]] is about two of our own signals contradicting each other. These do not contradict; they
  are both correct and both silent on the question.
- [[R029]] is false survivors from coverage ATTRIBUTION, where the mutant was never really
  covered. Here it was covered, and ran.

So the gap is real and unfiled until now: a survivor that executed, was attributed correctly, and
still is not a finding.

## Why it is not simply fixable, and what is worth doing

Deciding equivalence in general is undecidable, and even the tractable cases need dataflow the AST
layer does not have. Ruling on it per operator at generation time is the wrong place too, whether
`Counter := 0` -> `1` matters depends on the loop that reads `Counter`, not on the assignment.

Three things that would help without pretending to decide it:

1. **Name the operators whose survivors are likelier equivalent.** `remove-assignment`,
   `toggle-blank-string` and `shift-integer` all mutate a written VALUE, and all three spikes
   recorded equivalence as their known weakness. A survivor from one of those could carry a hint
   that says so, the way `platformArtifactKills` hints at a kill's mechanism without moving it.
2. **Report a survivor's re-execution difference.** If a mutant runs and produces byte-identical
   observable output to the baseline on every covering test, that is evidence FOR equivalence,
   cheaply, without any analysis. It is not proof, and must not be reported as proof.
3. **Let a reader mark one.** An equivalent mutant found by hand is knowledge worth keeping across
   runs, and the identity key from [[R166]] is stable enough to hang it on.

None of the three is started. The measured instance is what this row records.

## Scale

Unmeasured on a real corpus, and that is the first thing a fix needs. What is known: 435 of
`shift-integer`'s 677 sites are assignments, the shape that produced this instance, so the class is
not rare.

## Proposal 1 SHIPPED, 2026-08-27

`MutationOperator.equivalenceRisk` (engine `operator/interface.ts`) lets an operator declare that its
survivors are likelier than average to be equivalent, and `SessionReport.likelyEquivalentSurvivors`
groups them by risk with the prose from `EQUIVALENCE_RISK_EXPLANATIONS`. Modelled on
`platformArtifactKills`: a hint attached to a verdict that does not move it. Nothing is subtracted
from `mutationScore` and no verdict changed on any gate.

Two risks exist, and both are about a KIND of mutation rather than a guess about one mutant:

- **`value-rewrite`** — the operator rewrites a written or compared value, so where nothing
  downstream reads it the mutant is equivalent. Declared by `remove-assignment`,
  `toggle-blank-string` and `shift-integer`.
- **`loop-truncation`** — the operator bounds a loop, so where the covering test drives one
  iteration, truncating to one changes nothing. Declared by `loop-truncate`.

**Declared only where a spike MEASURED an equivalent survivor**, never wherever one seems plausible.
That restraint is the whole design: R175's first detector flagged every uncovered procedure in every
trigger-carrying table and had to be narrowed out of it, and a hint that fires on most survivors
retires the word "survivor" without replacing it.

**Calibration, measured on `fixtures/sandbox-app`: 2 of 12 survivors flagged**, and they are the two
already documented as equivalent-shaped, `shift-integer` and `remove-assignment` on `LogAudit`'s
self-assignment. Not 12, not 0.

**What is still not fixed.** Proposal 2 (report a survivor's re-execution difference as cheap
evidence FOR equivalence) and proposal 3 (let a reader mark one, keyed on [[R166]]'s stable
identity) are not started. Neither is a hint: they are the two that could tell a reader something
about a PARTICULAR mutant, which this one deliberately does not.

## Proposal 2 REFUSED 2026-08-29, and the reason is this project's signature bug

Proposal 2 reads: "If a mutant runs and produces byte-identical observable output to the baseline on
every covering test, that is evidence FOR equivalence, cheaply, without any analysis."

**Implemented as written it is vacuous, and it would fire on every survivor.** A survivor is BY
DEFINITION a mutant where no covering test failed, and the baseline is all-green, so the pass/fail
signal is identical for every survivor before anything is measured. Checked against what is actually
recorded per run rather than reasoned about:

| recorded on a `TestVerdict` | on a survivor's run vs the baseline |
| --- | --- |
| `outcome` | `pass` both sides, by the definition of `survived` |
| `failureMessage` | absent both sides |
| `coverage` | **not collected at all** — mutant runs pass `coverage: "none"` (`orchestrator.ts`, both the covering-run and confirm-run call sites) |
| `durationMs` | wall-clock noise, not an observable of the program |

So the comparison returns "identical" always, for every survivor, carrying no information. Shipping
it would attach a confident-sounding equivalence hint to 100% of survivors, which is exactly the
empty-vs-empty match `CLAUDE.md` names as this project's signature bug, and it would retire the word
"survivor" the same way proposal 1's calibration note warns against.

### The natural repair also fails, on this row's own instance

The non-vacuous version is to collect coverage on MUTANT runs too and diff the execution trace:
a trace that differs proves the mutant changed execution, and an identical trace is weak evidence for
equivalence. Two objections, and the second is decisive:

1. **Cost.** It is a coverage collection per (mutant x covering test) rather than once per baseline.
   On `fixtures/sandbox-data` that is 345 mutants against the fenced line-map path, which is the
   expensive half of a run today.
2. **It does not detect the instance this row was filed for.** `HangLogic:33`, `Counter := 0` ->
   `Counter := 1` inside `CountUpTo(3)`: from 0 the loop walks 1,2,3 and from 1 it walks 2,3. Both
   return 3 on every input, so the mutant is equivalent — and the loop runs a DIFFERENT number of
   times, so the execution trace differs. A trace-identity test reports "not equivalent" for the one
   mutant this row proved equivalent.

An instrument that costs the most expensive thing in the run and answers wrongly on the only case
that motivated it is not worth building. **Equivalence is a property of a program's OUTPUT over all
inputs, and an execution trace is not that.**

### What this leaves

Proposal 1 (shipped) hints at a CLASS. Proposal 3 records a HUMAN RULING about a particular mutant.
There is no cheap automatic middle, and this row should stop implying there is.

## Proposal 3 SHIPPED 2026-08-29

`lethal.equivalent.json`, discovered beside the project, records a reader's ruling that a particular
survivor cannot be killed, keyed on [[R166]]'s stable identity. `SessionReport.readerMarkedEquivalent`
reports how those rulings met this run.

**Nothing here moves a verdict or the score**, and that is the design rather than a limitation. A
mark is a human's reasoning, which LethAL cannot verify; a score that improved because someone edited
a JSON file would be strictly worse than no feature. Measured end to end on `fixtures/sandbox-app`:
`mutationScore` 15.8%, killed 3 / survived 16, byte-identical to the same run with no marks file.

### Three outcomes, and the third is the only one that proves anything

| outcome | meaning |
| --- | --- |
| `matched` | the mark names a mutant that survived here. The ordinary case. |
| `stale` | the mark names nothing here. The identity carries the mutated subtree's hash, so editing that code retires the mark rather than letting it drift onto a different mutant. Safe, but a ruling nobody is told they lost is not. |
| `contradicted` | the mark names a mutant this run did NOT let survive. Someone stated no test could distinguish it and a test did. **The verdict is the measurement, the mark is the opinion, and the verdict wins.** |

`no-coverage` counts as a contradiction, deliberately: a mutant nobody ran is not a mutant nobody
could kill, and conflating those is the confusion [[R175]] exists to prevent. `known-survivor` counts
as a survival, because it is a survivor carried from a prior run rather than a fresh refutation.

### Refusals built in

- **A mark with no `reason` is refused at parse time**, not defaulted. An unexplained mark is an
  unreviewable annotation on the one list a reader is supposed to act on.
- **A malformed file throws rather than loading partially.** A marks file that quietly loaded 4 of
  its 5 rulings would hide the fifth as "a survivor nobody has examined yet".
- **A duplicate key is refused** rather than last-wins.
- **An unreadable file is NOT treated as an absent one.** Absent means nobody has ruled on anything;
  a permissions error means every ruling was discarded, and they must not look alike.
- **Discovery is a fixed filename, not a flag.** A ruling is a property of the codebase, and a flag
  would let one run apply the rulings and the next silently not.

### Verified

Red-checked: neutering the contradiction branch reds exactly the two tests that pin it, 12 others
stay green. 18 unit tests. Then run END TO END through the real CLI against `sandbox-app` with a
marks file carrying one of each case, and all three fired.

**That end-to-end run is why it is worth doing rather than trusting the unit tests.** The wiring
patched only ONE of `orchestrator.ts`'s two `FoldStatics` assemblies — the quarantine/early-report
path — so every unit test passed, typecheck passed, and the feature did nothing on the normal path.
A pure-function test suite cannot see a field that is never handed to it.
