Measured by coord PR-103 (lethal-code, 2026-11-17) from DO window t12's 989 `serve-request-sized` events (head 4763e43d,
edge-847b1fa5a; `/coord/handoff/PR-103/measure.md`).

**The defect.** `noteEntryCost` charges each call a request's growth above the daemon's floor (`g`) divided by the request's
test entries, and keeps the highest value (max-only, R987). But `g` is nearly independent of the entry count:
- an OLS fit gives an intercept of 0.54-0.62 GiB and a slope of 2.6-3.2 MiB per entry, with R² 0.05-0.14;
- a 2-entry and a 200-entry request both grow the daemon by about 1 GiB.

Dividing by a small count gives hundreds of MiB per entry, and the cost never falls. In t12:
- 51 of 110 codeunits were capped below the 256-entry chunk cap, at caps from 4 to about 224.
- 47 of those 51 caps were set by a request of 20 entries or fewer, and 27 by one of 4 or fewer.
- The stderr cap lines print only the first lowering. Codeunit 68975 printed 32 entries at 125 MiB, but ended at 821 MiB and a
  cap of 4.

**The headroom left unused.** The largest prefetch request grew 1.22 GiB, 31% of `ENTRIES_REQUEST_BUDGET_BYTES` (4 GiB). The
largest absolute daemon peak was 4.37 GiB, against the default `daemonMaxRssGb` of 6.

**What it costs.** At most about 683 s of a t12-sized window's 8,553 s of prefetch (8%), if every request packed to the chunk
cap. The rest of t12's prefetch rise over t11 was work: 2.07x the tests. The cost matters more under PR-99's lanes, which share
the memory budget.

**What does not fix it.**
- A "clean readings only" rule: 80 of 110 codeunits never get a request of their own.
- One calibration request per codeunit: about 640 s, and its division still gives a large per-entry cost.

**The fix being built (coord PR-103).** A max-only AFFINE bound: a fixed jump plus a per-entry slope. Today's rule is kept for
any codeunit with fewer than 2 readings at different entry counts. A large-request memory check on DO's heaviest codeunits runs
before the bound is relied on.
