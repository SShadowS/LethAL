**Found by** coord PR-350 (lethal-code, 2026-11-22), attributing R1183 from c2's events
(`/coord/handoff/PR-350/findings.md`, `warmups.txt`).

**The size:**
- Every serve daemon start on full DO compiles for about 35 s.
- c2's daemons closed on a refusal 146 times, and 89 of them were ONE family: al-runner refuses "an error in a SaveAs of
  report …, which has a layout of this …" (al-runner's own text, shortened here).
- Each such close costs a fresh daemon, so about 89 × 35 s = 3,100 s of compile, plus the new daemon's lazy proofs.
- On t1 (the same build, PR-302) the same family leads, with 44 of 118 refusal closes.

**Why not keep after every refusal now:** that is R1162, blocked on upstream #8180's promise that a refusal leaves no
state behind. One family is a smaller claim, and it can be measured directly.

**To close it:**
1. **A direct kept-vs-fresh check on DO for this family** (PERF-15's method; the standing rule that a keep needs a direct DO
   check):
   - run the mutants whose requests hit this refusal on a daemon KEPT after it, and on a FRESH one, in the run's own
     isolation mode;
   - the census of rows must be equal.
2. **Then a keep-list entry for this text** (as PR-56's), pre-committed, with the R1152/R1153 lessons:
   - the anchored pattern matched against the text as al-runner ends it;
   - and leg C's `serve-keep-reasons` covering the entry.
3. **Then a full-DO run** must show the restarts cut, and verdicts equal per mutant against the run before.

**If upstream's compile cache lands first** (#8446, R1183), a restart becomes cheap, and this keep is worth mostly the
re-proofs. Re-size it then.
