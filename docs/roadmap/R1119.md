**What is missing.** PR-121(a) (spec `2026-11-17-pr121a-window-barriers.md`) measures window-barrier time with an offline script,
`/coord/handoff/PR-121/gaps.ts`, over a run's events (`atMs`, PR-121a): per lane, busy time, the gap before the first request,
inner gaps by size, and the gap after the last. Neither the end-of-run TIMING section (PR-100) nor any event carries these
figures. So a window's pre-commitment cannot be scored on barrier time from the run's own output: DO window x3 predicted "lane barrier
time at most 60 s" and TIMING printed nothing to score it with.

**What gaps.ts showed on x3** (`/coord/handoff/R-984/x3.run.events.ndjson`, edge-4ebfe7913, `--workers 3`, mutant phase 12,448 s):
- lane 0: 1,408 requests, busy 98.7%, inner gaps 156.6 s;
- lanes 1 and 2: 236 and 151 requests, busy 13.5% and 13.8%; **after their last request 10,605 s and 10,589 s (85% of the phase)**;
- inner gaps over 5 s: one, 5.3 s. So PR-121a's barrier is gone (the prediction held), but **the window ran on one lane for 85% of
  its mutant phase** and nothing in TIMING says so: its serve times are summed over lanes, which hides an idle lane.

**To close.** TIMING prints, per lane: busy time, the time after the lane's last request (idle at the end), and inner gaps over
5 s (barrier time); the same figures go into an event. Then a window's lane prediction is scored from its own output. Whether one
lane carrying 85% of a window is a scheduling defect (the remaining units all on one lane) is a separate question for lethal-code;
this item is the missing measurement.
