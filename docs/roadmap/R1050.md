Found by PR-34 (lethal-bugs, 2026-11-15; `/coord/handoff/PR-34/bc/run.md`), a bcdev run on a copy of System Application's Filter
Tokens module on bc2 (BC 28.4 W1 on Linux).

**The shape.** In `FilterTokensImpl`, a `while Length <> 0 do` loop advances only when the local procedure `ReadUntilCharacter`
moves its `var Position` argument forward. LethAL refuses deleting the call (R830: a call the loop's progress may rest on), and that
site was hang-refused. But `lethal.empty-block` on `ReadUntilCharacter`'s own body (mutant M0332) has the same effect from the
callee's side, and nothing refused it. al-runner v3 scored it timeout-killed. The pre-commitment meant to leave it out by line
scope, but `--lines` keeps a mutant that touches an allowed line, and its span did.

**What happened on BC.** The run had no `--stop-hung-sessions` (opt-in, default off, by design: R053, R191), so the control app's
stop was never asked for. LethAL's own 180 s timer aborted RunMutantMany ("our timer, not BC's stop"), quarantined the test
(in-flight-unknown) and left the control app's lease orphaned. The doctor's recovery is an NST restart, then
`lethal force-reset-lease`, so it needed the owner (kraken q-20261115T185041-55e86d96). This is the outcome `itest:hang`'s OFF leg
produces on purpose. **The stop did not fail**: it was not on. `itest:hang` covers the stop; it does not cover this refusal gap,
because its fixture has no callee-side shape.

**Why it matters.** A real project's helper that steps a caller's loop through a `var` parameter, emptied by `empty-block` (or by
removing its assignment, the R196 operators inside the callee), can strand a server for anyone running without
`--stop-hung-sessions`.

**Possible fix** (not designed): extend R830's analysis one step across a call. When a loop's progress rests on a `var` argument of
a call to a procedure in the same object, treat the writes to that parameter inside the callee (and an `empty-block` of its body)
as hang-capable sites, as R196 does for the loop's own body.

**To close.** An offline test with the invented shape (a loop stepped only by a local procedure's `var` write) shows the callee's
`empty-block` and its `remove-assignment` sites hang-refused, and red-checks when the cross-call step is removed.

**Fixed for `empty-block` (coord PR-37, 2026-11-15).** Pre-commitment `docs/superpowers/specs/2026-11-15-pr37-r1050-emptied-progress.md`
(master 833639d4); evidence `/coord/handoff/PR-37/`.
- **Ground truth** (an invented reduction, run on v3): 7 of 16 mutants hang, the callee body's `empty-block`, the callee's 5 other
  sites, and the caller's `empty-block` of the block holding the exit-feeding write. The premise above that "R830 refuses deleting the
  call" did not hold: a bare same-object call is not a `void-method-call` target.
- **Built** (`openItemHangRefuses`, `lethal.empty-block` only): `emptiedProgressCalleeHangs` refuses emptying a same-object callee's
  body when a call to it sits in a loop whose exit (`exitEffect`, which already follows the loop body, R818) reads a name the call can
  move (the callee chain's writes, or an argument bound to a `var` parameter); `emptiedRefusedBlockHangs` refuses emptying a block that
  holds an assignment to an exit-read name or a progress call (each already refused on its own). R830 itself is unchanged.
  After the Opus review, rule 1 also covers (pre-commitment addendum 1): a progress callee reached through another one (a per-object
  fixpoint), a value-returning callee whose result is assigned to an exit-read name, and one called in the loop's exit, unless its
  default return (0, false, '') provably ends that loop at once (the simple exit shapes; keeps `sandbox-hang`'s `until NextRow() = 0`).
  The review's two other shapes are [[R1052]].
- **Not built, by [[R837]]'s ruling:** the callee's other sites (its `remove-assignment`s, its own loop's `loop-skip` and conditions).
  They stay hang-capable on a BC server; the safety net there is the zero-timeout check and `--stop-hung-sessions` (PR-34 addendum 2).
- **The trade-off, accepted by the orchestrator for server safety:** on 72 BC.History modules the rules refuse 53 sites in 13 modules;
  against their earlier v3 runs, 4 were hangs (every `empty-block` hang in those stores) and 28 were scored mutants (25 killed, 3
  survived) that are now lost, about 1 hang per 8 refusals (R837's general rule was about 1 in 30; this item's first build, before
  the review fixes, 1 in 4). 8 other hangs remain there. Most of the review fixes' added cost is the transitive step in Filter Tokens
  (11 helper bodies, all killed): narrowing it is [[R1053]].
- Fixtures: two sites leave, both in `sandbox-optin`'s `OptinLogic`: `ResetCounter`'s body (not a hang: the R830-style
  over-approximation) and `Consume`'s body (`Remaining := Consume(Remaining)`; not a hang, R829's trade). The R-307 O8 emit golden
  re-recorded for that one entry by ruling (124 -> 122; 230 other entries and the reach-grain golden identical; normalized, only the two
  guards differ). `sandbox-hang` and every other fixture unchanged: no gate's mutant set moved.
- Tests: `packages/builtin-tier1/tests/r1050-emptied-progress.test.ts`, 18, red-checked (rule 1 off, rule 2 off and its call branch,
  the exit-read condition dropped, the `var`-only filter dropped, the fixpoint step, the return-value seed, the default-return check).
