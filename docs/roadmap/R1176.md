**Found by** coord PR-338 (lethal-code, 2026-11-22), offline from c2's process samples and events (`current` main-ccd0f823d,
`--peak 22`). Evidence: `/coord/handoff/PR-338/findings.md`, `rss.txt`, `tree.txt`.

**What a lane holds:**
- **The serve daemon:** 3.25 GiB median, 3.48 max. Its .NET sidecar is 0.05 GiB.
- **It is not a leak.** The RSS at a request's start, by the daemon's request number:
  - 2.59 GiB for requests 0-24, 2.71 for 50-74, 3.13 for 75-99;
  - flat at 3.21-3.24 from request 125 to 400.
- **The floor is set by the instrumented tree:** PR-274's 22-file slice peaked at 2.05 GiB per daemon. The rest is what a
  daemon retains (R1177).
- **LethAL's own process:** the gate charges a fixed 4.0 GiB (`LANE_LETHAL_BYTES`) for the whole run.
  - **CORRECTED by PR-339** (`/coord/handoff/PR-339/lethal-rss.txt`). PR-338 placed the 5.33 GiB peak in the baseline phase;
    it is in the MUTANT phase. Over that phase: median 2.06, p90 2.33, p99 3.41, max 5.33 GiB.
  - The spikes come throughout: 4.7 GiB at 17:17, 4.5 at 18:23, 5.3 at 19:23 (c2's clock).

**The rule today:** lanes × 3.4 + 4.0 ≤ 22, which gives 5 lanes.

**The LethAL-owned levers, sized at c2's figures:**
- **A. Recycle a daemon before the step:** about 2.75 GiB per lane, so 6 lanes at `--peak 22`.
  - **The cost:** about 14 extra warm-ups per lane at about 48 s each, 5.6% of a lane. The net is about +13%.
  - **It already exists** (PR-339 found it): `alRunnerV3.daemonMaxRssGb` (default 6) and `alRunnerV3.daemonMaxRequests`
    (default 0, off), from R952.
    - `ensureDaemon` replaces a daemon past either bound (`overRecycleBound`, then `closeLane(..., "recycle")`).
    - The new daemon is warmed up and checked against the baseline like any restart.
    - `daemonMaxRssGb 2.9` fits better than a request count: it recycles when the step arrives, on any project.
- **B. Charge LethAL's live RSS: WITHDRAWN as unsafe** (PR-339). LethAL's own RSS spikes to 4.7-5.3 GiB inside the mutant
  phase. A live charge would start lanes on a low reading, and the next spike would push the run over its declared peak.
  The fixed 4.0 GiB is not generous; it is slightly under.
- **The verdict claim to keep:** a recycled daemon answers as a fresh one (PERF-15; daemons already restart 168 times in
  c2, 146 of them on refusals). Check it per mutant against c2 in the A/B.

**Not LethAL's, recorded here for sizing:**
- **C.** A declared peak of 28 (`memory.max` 32 minus the 4 GiB reserve) gives 7 lanes with no code. It is box policy: the
  owner or orchestrator.
- **D.** al-runner releasing the retained 0.6 GiB is R1177. The answer: the step is reached code, kept for the daemon's
  life, so recycling (A) is the surest lever. A mimalloc purge setting saves only about 0.15 GiB for +1.7% instructions.
  It is a third A/B option only if A lands near the bound.
- **E.** A smaller instrumented tree per daemon (about 2.1 GiB, as the slice) would give 8 lanes. It is large: per-window
  artifacts and compiles. Not sized further.

**Related:** R1177, R1156 (the instrumented tree's size), R1147 (keeping the daemon after a timeout), PR-274 (no
self-contention up to 8 lanes).
