Where a fixture puts the three objects LethAL injects (`Mutation Selector`, `Mutation Register`,
`Mutation Upgrade`) is decided per fixture, and the four here do it two different ways:

| fixture | declared `idRanges` | its own objects | injected triple | placement |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| `sandbox-app` | 79000-79199 | 79000-79001 | 79197-79199 | top three of the range it already declares |
| `sandbox-data` | 79300-79399 | 79300-79331 | 79397-79399 | top three of the range it already declares |
| `sandbox-hang` | 79400-79449 | 79400 | 79447-79449 | top three of the range it already declares |
| `gift-card` | 90100-90149 **and** 90197-90199 | 90100-90103 | 90197-90199 | a SEPARATE three-id range declared for the purpose |

The user's report named the digit patterns (`x197-x199` twice, then `79447-79449`, then
`90197-90199`) as the inconsistency. Measured, the digits are a red herring: `79447-79449` IS the top
three of `79400-79449`, so `sandbox-hang` follows the same rule as App and Data. **The real split is
that three fixtures carve the triple out of a range they also fill with their own objects, and
`gift-card` gives it a range of its own.**

**`gift-card` has the better rule and it is the minority.** A triple at the top of a working range
is safe only while the app has not grown into it. `sandbox-data` currently reaches 79331 against a
triple at 79397, so it has room now; the failure mode is a fixture growing 66 more objects and
landing on its own selector, which produces a compile error at publish time about an object the
author never wrote. A dedicated three-id range cannot be grown into.

**A second, smaller inconsistency: the roles are not in the same order.** App, Data and `gift-card`
put `selectorId` on the TOP id of the three and `tableId` on the bottom; `sandbox-hang` reverses it
(`selectorId: 79447`, `tableId: 79449`). Both are legal, and nothing reads the ordering. It matters
only because someone writing a fourth config by reading a third will map the roles by position.

**Why this is a row and not a fix now.** After [[R169]] nothing collides, and renumbering
`sandbox-hang` would churn a live gate's fixture to buy tidiness. What is missing is the written
rule, and the rule is what a real user needs: `examples/gift-card/README.md` has to explain the
`selectorIds` block to anyone pointing LethAL at their own app, and today it can only say "this app
reserves 90197..90199" instead of naming the practice.

**What a fix looks like:** write the convention down once (declare a dedicated three-id range,
never borrow a neighbour's and never share with objects you will grow into), state it in
`fixtures/README.md` and in the gift-card README's `selectorIds` paragraph, and extend
`validateSelectorIdsForProject` to refuse selector ids that fall outside the target's OWN declared
`idRanges`. That last part is the load-bearing half, and note what it would and would not have
caught: under [[R169]]'s three-id island the ids WERE inside a declared range, so this check would
have passed. It catches the cheaper mistake of pointing at ids the app never reserved. Catching
[[R169]]'s shape needs a different check, that no two fixtures declare the same triple.

Reported by the user 2026-08-20 while reviewing R169's move.
