**What.** The hang rules look at loops. `if Level < 10 then Self(Level + 1)`, with the guard forced true or the depth argument blanked or swapped, recurses without bound, and nothing refuses it. Found by the opus plan review of R-862, in the code, on the prototype. Parse counts of self-calling arms for `condition-true`: DO 2, DC 4.

**Shipped too.** The review found one such shape under the shipped `swap-additive`. Not counted across operators.

**The probe was dropped, 2026-11-08, by the owner's ruling,** before any run: `2026-11-08-r865-recursion-probe-precommitment.md`. ## The narrow rule (coord R-871, the owner's pick)

**Cost, counted by parse.** On 2.71 million specs, a refusal of anything on a direct recursion's guard, argument or base case would refuse 2,866 specs: cdo 45, do 83, dc 258, bch 2,480, and 0 in the fixtures and `examples/`. An opus agent read a stratified sample of 42: 6 could plausibly recurse without bound, 3 were unclear, and 33 still end. 19 of the 42 were calls to an overload of the same name, not recursion. So the owner picked a narrow rule.

**The rule.** `recursionDepthHangs` in `recursion-hazard.ts` refuses `swap-additive` and `delete-additive-*` on a **depth argument**: an argument of a direct self-call (`Name(...)` or `this.Name(...)`) whose parameter a guard reads, directly or through other arguments. A guard is the condition of an `if` whose arm holds a self-call or an `exit`. An overload is never recursion: the call must pass as many arguments as the procedure has parameters. When another procedure of that name and count exists, every argument's type must be known and match this procedure only. The parse count was 24 specs in 11 procedures (bch 22, dc 2). In the sample this group was 4 unbounded, 1 unclear and 1 that still ends.

**Named limits: not refused.**
- A value that stops shrinking through an assignment. Seen in the sample: a `remove-assignment` that keeps a text argument at the same length.
- A guard that calls again in the same state. Seen in the sample: a negated confirm that reopens a page and recurses.
- Mutual recursion: A calls B, and B calls A.
- Recursion that counts its depth in globals, not in an argument.
- Recursion through another object.
- A procedure split by `#if` (`isProcedureLike`'s split forms): the rule looks only for a plain `procedure`.
- A procedure written twice with one signature, once in each arm of a `#if/#else`. Two twins of the same count are found, the type table gives no type inside `#if` arms, and the overload check then says "not recursion".
- A counter that guards only against cycles valid data cannot have, such as certified BOMs. Such a mutant still ends on real data, and the rule refuses it anyway.

**Closed by the owner's pick, with the limits above.** The narrow rule is the whole fix. A mutant on a recursion's guard stays claimed for the shipped operators: in the sample, 1 of 14 guard mutants was unbounded and 12 still end. The opt-in `condition-true` refuses its own self-call sites (R868). The guard case and every limit above are named, not open work. A hang there costs a timeout and quarantine, and what it does on BC is not measured.
