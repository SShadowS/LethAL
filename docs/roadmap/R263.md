**Measured, not inferred, but the gap between the two containers is not explained.** The facts
below come from `docs/superpowers/specs/2026-09-26-r236-rate-precommitment.md`'s OUTCOME and
addenda, and from `docs/roadmap/R236.md`'s new read-out; see those two for the full detail.

**Cronus28, pre-arm smokes.** The traced smoke hit 4 of 5 sessions; the untraced smoke hit 2 of 2.
Together, 6 lost replies in 7 pre-arm sessions on Cronus28. Of the 5 hits (traced and untraced) that
were followed by a preflight check, 2 failed it and needed a container restart, a wedge.

**Cronus284, the counted arms.** Arm A hit 3 of 30 counted sessions, arm B' hit 3 of 30 counted
sessions: 6 of 60 counted sessions overall. The Fisher test between the two arms found no
measurable increase (p = 0.6646). Of the 3 warm-up sessions (the first two on the container, and
the first after its one restart), 2 hit. Wedges were rarer here too: 1 of 8 hits (6 counted, 2
warm-up) was followed by a failed preflight, against Cronus28's 2 of 5.

**What every hit looked like.** On Cronus284, every one of the 8 hits (6 counted, 2 warm-up) has a
partial-body capture: response headers arrived, then part of the body, then either the client's
timeout or a socket close, with the end of the answer missing, a truncated reply with byte counts
for each. On Cronus28, the four TRACED hits showed the same partial-body pattern with end-of-answer
cuts (byte counts recorded); the two UNTRACED hits have no byte counts, only the client's timeout or
socket-close outcome, so they are lost replies but not themselves evidenced as truncated. Headers
arrived later on every Cronus284 hit (606 ms or more) than on every clean session (543 ms at most);
the one clean Cronus28 smoke session showed the same pattern, headers well ahead of every hit's. The
truncation-decision pre-commitment could not say whether the loss sits on the server's send side or
the client's: neither the consistent-offset nor the otherwise-identical condition held.

**Candidate differences between the two containers, stated as open questions only, none of them
measured:**
- Container age and uptime, and prior load: Cronus28 had run many gates the day of the smokes;
  Cronus284 was freshly provisioned for this measurement.
- Resident apps: Cronus28 carried old instrumented residents and extra control-app versions;
  Cronus284's residents were cleared before its arms ran.
- BC build: Cronus28 was on 28.0.53667.0. Cronus284's build is not recorded in the
  precommitment spec's logs.
- Keep-alive socket reuse: no per-request socket identity was recorded on either container, so this
  cannot be read from what already exists.

**What closing this needs.** A pre-committed measurement that varies one of the candidate
differences at a time, on a matched pair of containers, and counts the truncation rate on each
side. Nothing measured so far licenses picking one candidate over another.

See R236 (the frozen quarantine mechanism, and the rate measurement this row is drawn from) and
R225 (the first observation of a fresh container's TestPage baseline going bad).
