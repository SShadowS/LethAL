**Filed 2026-10-10 by the [[R571]] measurement** (`/coord/handoff/R-571/measure.md`, coord task R-571; orchestrator
ruling of the same day). An OVER-refusal: mutants that cannot hang are refused. That direction is safe, but it
costs mutants.

**What.** R531 refuses the consumers of a cursor loop's record R inside the loop body. When it follows a call
into a procedure of the same object, it can match a record in that procedure to R by name (the alias-by-name
rule). It does this even when one side is declared `temporary` and the other is a real-table record. Those are
different storage. A Delete or Modify on the real table cannot move a loop that walks the temporary table, and the
reverse holds too.

**Seen in the R-571 measurement.** BaseApp `Reservation Management`.MatchSurplus was reached through the
alias-by-name rule from a loop over a temporary table. MatchSurplus's own records are real-table records. Read by
hand: it cannot hang. R-571's cross-object hop skips this case (see R571). The same-object hop was not changed and
was not measured.

**To close.** In R531's same-object hop, skip an alias only when BOTH flags are known and differ: an explicit
`temporary` on one side and a real-table declaration on the other. An unknown flag is not skipped, and a by-value
temporary record stays refused as R531 ruled. Measure the corpus price, per mutant, and say whether any ordinal
moves.
