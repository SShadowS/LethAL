Found by coord PR-251 (lethal-code, 2026-11-20) and CORRECTED by PR-273 the same day. Evidence: `/coord/handoff/PR-251/`
(`result.md`, `gap.txt`, `gap-split.txt`, `overlap.txt`) and `/coord/handoff/PR-273/result.md` §0. Source: perf10c
(`/coord/handoff/PR-217/perf10c.run.events.ndjson`, `perf10c.procs.tsv`).

**What PR-251 saw.** Prefetch wall minus every al-runner timer summed to 6,972 lane-s:
- **About 5,900 lane-s came from one prefetch request per lane** that seemed open about 1,990 s, from about 13.06M to
  15.06M ms. Its al-runner timers explain only 20-32 s, and its untimed part was IDENTICAL on all three lanes (1,967.19 s).
- **1,515 lane-s are requests cut at a timeout:** no timings, about 60 s each. That is real time, R1147's lever.
- **About 18 lane-s are left** (median per-request gap 1 ms).

PR-251 read the 5,900 lane-s as overlapping requests (a second daemon per lane, and a fixed-length wait) and proposed
clipping overlaps in every per-lane sum.

**What it actually was (PR-273): server1 slept during the run.**
- **The process sampler (every 2 s) jumps 1,970 s,** from 06:46:06 to 07:18:56Z.
- **`session-finished` reports `elapsedMs` 20,716,718 against `atMs` 18,751,326,** a gap of 1,965 s.
- **So the two clocks differ:**
  - event `atMs` is monotonic, and leaves the sleep out;
  - request `wallMs` and phase `elapsedMs` were `Date.now` differences (before PR-252), and include it.
- **The three requests were simply in flight when the machine slept.**
  - Their `wallMs` holds the 1,965 s, hence the identical untimed part on every lane.
  - Their start computed as `atMs - wallMs` lands 33 minutes too early. That is why they appeared to "overlap" 377-464 later
    requests, and why each lane's summed request wall exceeded the mutant phase.
- **Not real:** there was no second-daemon wait and no 33-minute memory hold. The mutant phase was 16,463 s by `atMs`.

**The rule (for event files written before PR-252).** Never build an interval from `atMs - wallMs`, and never sum request
walls, across a suspend. Find suspends by a wall-clock jump in a sampler, or by `elapsedMs` minus the `atMs` span. Then take
times from al-runner's own timers or from `atMs`. Runs on PR-252's head measure durations on the monotonic clock, and PR-264
prints `run-suspended` when the machine sleeps, so the cause is closed for new runs.
