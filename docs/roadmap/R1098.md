Measured by coord PR-101 (lethal-code, 2026-11-17, measure only), on the same slice as [[R1097]] (`/coord/handoff/PR-101/`).

**What happens.** v3's request loop has no break on a failing test (`corpus.rs`, the entry loop). The only stop from outside
is a cancel. So after a mutant's first failing test, everything after it still runs:
- the rest of its entry: 1,110 tests, 28.6 s;
- the mutant's later entries (other covering codeunits): 427 passes, 164 s, of which 113 s are tests and 51 s are the per-pass
  floor.

That is 193 s together, 10.7% of the slice's serve time, and about 770 s on a t11-sized window (an estimate).

**Ordering helps only with a stop.** The killing codeunit is already the mutant's first entry for 177 of 265 kills (median 1,
p90 5). Inside its entry, the killer's place is median 2, p90 8. A kill-first order together with a stop would save at most
291 s (16.1%). Without a stop it saves nothing.

**Why LethAL cannot do it alone.** One request per entry would cost 0.25-0.66 s of request setup each (`setupMs`), more than
it saves.

**The lever.** Ask upstream for a per-entry or per-group stop at the first failing test, for example on a group of entries
that belong to one mutant. Then send each mutant's entries kill-likely first. The kill semantics must keep R197's ledger rule,
by which `killingTest` may change with the order.

**Upstream sweep (PR-240, 2026-11-19):** #8210 closed by PR 8484 (f7b156fb4, `stopAfterFirstFailure`, scoped to the
request), on upstream main, not in `current` (4ebfe7913). Class B: decided with [[R1146]]'s re-measure at the first pin
move past it. `docs/measurements/2026-11-19-pr240-upstream-sweep.md`.
