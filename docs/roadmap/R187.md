**Two paths on this machine hold one corpus.** `U:/Git/do-lethal/Cloud` and `U:/Git/do-rel2/Cloud`
are byte-identical: 554 `.al` files each, and one md5 over the sorted per-file hashes of all
non-dependency AL. The retrodiction reproduces to the digit on both (417 parsed, 29,078 distinct
edits, `swap-rec-xrec` 36, `loop-skip` 23).

**The hazard is confirmation, not correctness.** Every measurement taken on either is sound; what is
unsound is treating a result from one as independent of a result from the other. That nearly
happened: [[R013]]'s admission rule was to be validated by re-running its retrodiction on
`do-lethal/Cloud` as "a second corpus", which would have returned the same numbers and read as the
floor being corpus-independent. It is not — on `DC/Cloud` the smallest ground-1 operator is 44, or 0
counting `swap-enum-member`, against 36 here.

**Where the confusion comes from.** Rows cite both paths for different purposes without either
saying they are the same tree: [[R174]]'s probe was measured on `do-lethal/Cloud`, while [[R181]],
[[R013]] and the R13 census cite `do-rel2/Cloud`. Read together they look like two projects.

**Corpora that ARE distinct and available**, so the next cross-check has somewhere real to go:
`U:/Git/DC/Cloud` (Continia Document Capture, 475 files, 29,940 edits) and
`U:/Git/BusinessCentral.Sentinel` (different vendor, 67 files, 1,337 edits). Both were measured
2026-08-31 and their figures are on [[R181]].

**The shape of the fix, not a prescription.** Either delete or clearly label one path, or record the
equivalence somewhere a measurement author reads before choosing a corpus. A one-line note in
`docs/measurements/README.md` naming the pair and the two genuinely distinct corpora would do it.
What must not happen is another row citing the two as independent evidence.

## OUTCOME, appended after the fix. Nothing above is edited.

**The root cause is sharper than "two paths, one corpus": they are two worktrees of one repository
at one commit.** `git worktree list` in either shows both, at `5f2a71d3`, on different branches
(`lethal/campaign-2026-08-03` and a `development/...-promotion` branch). So they are identical by
construction today and will diverge the instant either branch moves — which means the fix this row
first proposed, a note saying they are the same, would itself have gone stale silently.

Fixed by making the identity computed rather than remembered:

- `scripts/corpus-fingerprint.ts` hashes exactly the files the instruments parse (`.al`, excluding
  `.dependencies`, via the same `corpusEntries` the retrodiction now uses, so the two cannot drift).
  Pinned by test: the same tree at two paths is one corpus, a one-byte change is a different one,
  `.dependencies` is invisible, a moved file is a different corpus.
- `scripts/r181-effect-grain-retrodiction.ts` prints the fingerprint as its FIRST line, the way the
  al-runner gate prints the build it ran against. Two runs printing the same hash measured the same
  corpus, whatever their paths say. Both DO paths print `9a8e8831449208cc`; `DC/Cloud` prints
  `dcad155c4ecdb38e`.
- [[R013]] now pins its reference corpus by full sha256, which is the half of the review's first
  finding ("name plus content hash") that the 2026-08-31 amendment only half did.
- `docs/measurements/README.md` tables all four corpora on this machine by hash, with the two DO
  paths marked as one.

Deliberately NOT done: deleting or renaming either worktree. Both are the user's checkouts of a
customer repository, one holds campaign artifacts, and a fix inside this repo must not reach into
them. The hash makes the duplication harmless rather than making it go away.
