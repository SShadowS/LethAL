**Found by reading, while planning C02-01 (2026-09-25).** `serializeKey`
(`packages/runner/src/selection.ts`) appends `|<ordinal>` when `ordinal > 0`, which is how
R193 tells identical twins apart. `parseEquivalenceMarks` refuses any key whose
`key.split("|").length !== 5` (`parseEquivalenceMarks`, `packages/runner/src/equivalence-marks.ts`).

**Consequence:** the second and later twins of one shape have a 6-field key, so a mark for one of
them is rejected when `lethal.equivalent.json` is parsed. The first twin can be marked; its twins
cannot, whatever the reader decided about them.

**Fix shape:** accept 5 fields, or 6 when the sixth is a positive integer, and keep refusing
anything else. Red-check: a test marking a twin with ordinal 1 that goes red with the old length
check.

Not measured live.

**Closed (d00ce37f).** `parseEquivalenceMarks` accepts exactly what `serializeKey` writes: 5
fields, or 6 when the sixth is a positive integer with no leading zero, sign or decimal. Anything
else still throws `EquivalenceMarksError`. `packages/runner/tests/equivalence-marks-report.test.ts`
marks the ordinal-1 twin of two identical statements through the real path (real operators and
writer, the marks file loaded from disk, `buildReport`) and asserts it marks that twin and not the
first; it goes red with the old length check. The agent reference now states the sixth-field rule.
