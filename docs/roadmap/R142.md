R137 gave `runConformance` a refusal contract: a case whose `expectedSpecs` is EMPTY now fails if the
operator produces anything. That closes the empty-vs-empty shape, and it found a refusal that had
never fired.

The other half is still open. For a case that expects, say, one spec, the runner checks only that the
expected spec APPEARED. An operator that emitted the expected spec plus a second, unwanted one at the
same snippet passes. Every non-empty case in `builtin-tier1` and `builtin-tier2` inherits that.

Why it was not closed with R137: the two are different contracts. "Emit nothing here" is unambiguous.
"Emit exactly this and nothing else" is a claim about a whole snippet, and the goldens were written
as spot checks — several deliberately name one interesting mutation in a snippet that also produces
ordinary `empty-block` or `return-value` mutants from OTHER operators. That is not a problem for the
per-operator runner (it only ever runs one operator), but it does mean nobody has checked whether the
existing goldens are exhaustive for their own operator. Turning the check on without that check first
would produce failures that are golden-under-specification, not operator bugs, and the temptation
would then be to weaken the contract rather than complete the goldens.

What would close this: run every operator's non-empty cases with an exactness check, list the cases
where produced exceeds expected, and either complete the expectation or record why the extra is
correct. Then make the check permanent. Cheap to measure, and the measurement decides the cost.

Evidence: `packages/operator-sdk/src/conformance.ts` (the `expectedRemaining` loop drains expected
against produced and never inspects the leftovers on the produced side), and [R137](R137.md).

## Closed 2026-08-14

**Measured first, exactly as the row asked.** `scripts/r142-probe/measure.ts` re-runs the harness's
own walk and its own matching rule over every registered operator and prints any produced spec the
golden does not account for:

    total cases 36, empty (refusal) cases 5, non-empty cases with EXTRA specs 0

All 15 registered operators, tier 1 and tier 2. Thirty-one non-empty cases and not one over-produces.
So the cost the measurement was supposed to decide turned out to be zero: no golden needed
completing, no operator bug was hiding behind the gap, and the check could be turned on as a contract
rather than a migration. The row's worry was still the right one to have — several snippets DO
produce mutations beyond the case's own, but from OTHER operators, and the per-operator runner never
sees those.

**The check is now permanent.** `runConformance` collects the produced specs that drained no
expectation and fails the case naming them. Two deliberate properties:

- It is reported SEPARATELY from "expected mutation not produced". A case can be both at once, and
  folding them into one message would let the second hide behind the first.
- It COUNTS rather than set-matches, so the same mutation emitted twice against a single expectation
  fails too.

**Red-checked on a real operator**, the way R137 was: `lethal.empty-block` was made to emit a second,
unwanted spec, and `tier 1 conformance` went red with
`case produced 1 spec(s) its expectation does not name: "begin DoThing(); end -> begin exit end"`.
Restored, 6 pass. The harness-level contract has its own stub-operator tests alongside, including the
control that an exactly-matching non-empty case still passes — a contract no correct operator can
satisfy would be worse than none.
