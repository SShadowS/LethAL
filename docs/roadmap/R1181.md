**Why** (R1176, closed): on full DO the gate charges every lane the session's MAX daemon reading (about 3.3 GiB), so
5 lanes fit at `--peak 22` (5 × 3.4 + 4 = 21). No existing knob changes that:
- the size bound turns itself off;
- request recycling alone leaves the max where it is.

**The measured inputs** (c2, `/coord/handoff/PR-348/post-warmup.txt` and `hwm-by-age.txt`; a request's high-water mark,
by daemon age):

| daemon age | median | p90 | p99 | max |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| requests 1-59 (young) | 2.63 | 3.15 | 3.30 | 3.33 |
| requests 100+ (old) | 3.23 | 3.25 | 3.30 | 3.30 |

**What a per-lane charge buys, and only with recycling:**
- **Old daemons:** 6 × 3.23 + 4 = 23.4 GiB, over 22 nearly all the time. Without recycling a sixth lane would be retired
  again soon after it started.
- **Young daemons** (`alRunnerV3.daemonMaxRequests` about 60, so no daemon gets old): 6 × 2.63 + 4 = 19.8 GiB at the
  median.
  - Six lanes are rarely all at p90 at once; at about 0.4 GiB spread per lane, the sum's p95 is near 21.5.
  - So a sixth lane fits most of the time, and the guard sheds it in the rare peaks.
- **The gain:** at most +20% lanes (5 to 6), less the recycling warm-ups (about 5.6% of a lane, R1176) and any lane
  retirements. That is about +10-13%, not measured. A seventh lane (7 × 2.63 + 4 = 22.4) does not fit.

**The design:**
1. **Admission:** a lane starts only while the SUM of the running lanes' current daemon readings, plus the new lane's
   estimate (the young p90, 3.15 GiB, or the session's own young p90 once measured), plus LethAL's fixed 4 GiB, is at or
   below the declared peak.
   - The 4 GiB stays fixed: LethAL itself spikes to 4.7-5.3 GiB inside the mutant phase (PR-339).
   - The cgroup rule (real use) is unchanged.
2. **Recycling:** `daemonMaxRequests` about 60, always set with this mode, so every daemon stays young.
3. **The guard, when the real total grows past its charge:** read at every request boundary. When the sum of daemon
   readings plus 4 GiB passes the declared peak:
   - hold: start no lane and restart no daemon;
   - then mark the NEWEST lane to retire after its current unit. Never kill a daemon mid-request, so no verdict can move.
   - Its remaining units go back to the queue for the other lanes.
   - Retired lanes may start again once the total is below the peak by one estimate.
   - The existing cgroup memory stop stays the hard backstop.
4. **Events and counts:** lanes started, holds, retirements and restarts, each with the readings that decided it. A run
   that retires often is visible in its report.

**The risks:**
- **OOM: low, not nil.**
  - The worst simultaneous case is 6 × 3.33 + 5.33 (LethAL's spike) = 25.3 GiB, against the cgroup's room of 28 GiB
    (32 less the 4 GiB reserve). So the cgroup is overrun only if other memory arrives at the same moment.
  - Other heavy-slot jobs are admitted on `memory.current` plus their peak (R972), so they wait rather than pile on.
  - Load outside the heavy-slot gate (alrunner's own work) is the open door.
- **Fairness:** the run may sit above its declared peak for seconds at a time. The heavy-slot bookkeeping (declared
  peaks) then understates it; `memory.current` does not.
- **Correctness:** lanes are a cost knob. Retiring a lane only after its unit, and re-queuing the rest, cannot move a
  verdict, provided the queue really re-queues. That is the first thing a build must check: can a lane leave mid-run
  today (`parallel-queue.ts`)?

**To decide:** build it (a pre-committed design, red tests on stateful fakes, then a full-DO A/B against today's gate,
verdicts equal per mutant), or keep 5 lanes. The alternative with no code is box policy, R1176's lever C (`--peak 28`),
which the orchestrator ruled out for now.
