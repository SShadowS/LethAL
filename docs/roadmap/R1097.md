Measured by coord PR-101 (lethal-code, 2026-11-17, measure only), on the DO e-document slice: 864 mutants, `--workers 1`,
c888648f, edge-4ebfe7913, events and report in `/coord/handoff/PR-94/pr100/`. The analysis and its scripts are in
`/coord/handoff/PR-101/result.md`.

**What happens.** `sendKillPair` sends a NONE entry and a mutant entry for the killing test's codeunit in one live request.
- The mutant entry carries L4's cut (`cutAfterLastCovering`).
- The NONE entry has no covering tests, so it runs the whole codeunit.

On the slice, the 1,248 pair entries ran 5,986 tests and took 376 s. 301 s of that was the NONE side.

**The lever.**
- Cut both entries of a pair after the killing test: about 284 s, 15.7% of the slice's 1,809 s serve time (about 1,140 s on a
  t11-sized window, scaled by serve seconds, an estimate). Cutting only the NONE entry to the mutant's prefix gives about 227 s
  (12.6%).
- Soundness rests on the same rule as PR-94: state flows forward only, and a `runSelectedOnly` prefix gives the full run's
  first rows (R1094).

**The open questions for a spec.**
- Which rows the confirm reads:
  - the killing test's row;
  - R998's left-out run;
  - any rule that compares a NONE row past the killer, such as a drift check (PR-56) or `suiteMissing`.
- Whether the batched pairs (`batchKillPairs`) share the shape.

**Smaller, filed here and not alone.** The echo entry per covered mutant costs 88 s (4.8%). It is just below the 5% bar for
an item of its own.
