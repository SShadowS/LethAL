**A survivor can carry `executionProven: true` beside `guardEvidence: "not-observed"` — two of our own signals flatly contradicting each other — and nothing says so.** `executionProven` is derived from `attribution === "exact"`, i.e. coverage claims a test executed the mutated procedure. `not-observed` is the DECISIVE guard-evidence state: the run's own attestation that no guarded site executed at all, whose shipped interpretation says the mutant "belongs with `no-coverage`". Both can appear on the same survivor, and `lethal explain` emits them side by side without remarking on the conflict — on the one command whose stated job is saying what is proven and what is not. Measured: **zero** `not-observed` survivors across all six committed campaign reports, and no fixture builds the pair, so this is latent rather than live — which is precisely why it needs filing rather than discovering later on a customer's report. The design question, deliberately not settled inside a fix wave: should the projection STATE the contradiction (and if so, keyed on what — a new field, or a tool-condition, since an interpretation may exist only if it is keyed to an existing machine value), or should the disagreement be reconciled upstream where attribution and guard evidence are decided, so it can never reach a report? Note the second option is the stronger one if the two signals are genuinely never both right, and the first is correct if they can legitimately disagree and the reader needs to know. A fixture pinning that the pair CAN occur and that the projection currently does not reconcile it landed with subsystem B; this row is about what to do next. Filed 2026-08-06 from subsystem B's whole-branch review.
