**The question this answers.** Every operator LethAL ships was justified one at a time, and every
operator it REFUSED has its reason written down, `RemoveSetLoadFields` on cost after a live
measurement, the three Tier-3 candidates in `docs/superpowers/specs/2026-08-02-r13-tier3-decision.md`.
What was never written down is the complement: the AL constructs no operator inspects **at all**,
which no refusal covers because nobody ever proposed them. A brainstormed list of "operators we
could add" is unfalsifiable. A histogram of the grammar's own node kinds over a real corpus, marked
with which kinds an operator's `targets()` claims, is not.

`scripts/census-node-kind-coverage.ts` is that instrument. It reads RAW tree-sitter node types, never
`ALNodeKind`: the curated enum is a subset chosen by us, so censusing it could only ever report that
the kinds we named are the kinds we handle, the blind spot would be invisible in exactly the shape
it takes.

**Measured 2026-08-19 on `do-rel2/Cloud`** (Continia Document Output, 554 `.al` files), counting only
nodes with a `procedure` or `trigger_declaration` ancestor:

| | |
| --- | --- |
| distinct node kinds inside procedure/trigger bodies | 115 |
| behaviour-carrying kinds (name ends `_statement` / `_expression`, or a literal) | 31 |
| of those, claimed by at least one operator | **5** |
| of those, never claimed by any operator | **26**, 26,865 occurrences |

The narrowing to 31 is mechanical, on the grammar's own naming convention, rather than a
hand-curated taste list: quoting the raw "109 of 115 kinds unclaimed" would count `end_keyword`,
`type_specification` and `comment` as mutation gaps and overstate the hole.

The five claimed kinds and what claims them:

| kind | executable | claimed | by |
| --- | ---: | ---: | --- |
| `call_expression` | 17,929 | 9,666 | ten operators |
| `comparison_expression` | 2,182 | 2,182 | `conditional-boundary`, `negate-conditional` |
| `logical_expression` | 641 | 641 | `negate-conditional` |
| `exit_statement` | 2,421 | 952 | `return-value` |
| `member_expression` | 19,114 | **42** | `swap-rec-xrec` only |

`code_block` sits outside the behavioural filter but matters to the reading below: `empty-block`
claims 6,100 of 6,562.

The 26 unclaimed, largest first: `assignment_statement` 6,850 · `string_literal` 4,892 ·
`if_statement` 4,389 · `boolean` 3,620 · `integer` 3,016 · `unary_expression` 1,124 ·
`additive_expression` 1,087 · `parenthesized_expression` 763 · `repeat_statement` 334 ·
`case_statement` 310 · `subscript_expression` 143 · `date_literal` 69 · `for_statement` 46 ·
`datetime_literal` 44 · `in_expression` 42 · `multiplicative_expression` 34 · `while_statement` 33 ·
`empty_statement` 23 · `foreach_statement` 13 · `time_literal` 12 · `ternary_expression` 6 ·
`call_statement` 5 · `decimal` 3 · `verbatim_string` 3 · `break_statement` 2 · `continue_statement` 2.

**Three things this number is NOT, stated so nobody quotes it as a coverage score.**

1. **Node-kind coverage is not mutation adequacy.** Two operators at different kinds can be killed
   by the same test, and this project has already refused an operator on exactly that ground:
   `IsolationLevelSwap` was refused because 25 of its 36 sites already carried a shipped
   `void-method-call` mutant. Overlap has to be measured per candidate, not assumed away.
2. **`empty-block` destroys the contents of 6,100 blocks**, so the code inside an unclaimed
   `assignment_statement` is not untested, it is untested *individually*. A whole-block deletion is
   a coarse mutant that a single weak assertion kills; the fine-grained one is what separates suites.
   The gap is real but it is a gap in DISCRIMINATION, not in reach.
3. **One corpus, one vendor.** A different app's mix will differ; `ternary_expression` at 6 here may
   be common elsewhere.

**The one entry with no recorded reason at all is arithmetic.** `additive_expression` (1,087) and
`multiplicative_expression` (34) are 1,121 sites of a construct that Arithmetic Operator Replacement
`+`↔`-`, `*`↔`/`, is the single most standard operator for in the mutation-testing literature, and
it is a staple of every comparable tool. `design.md` §4 does not list it in Tier 1, Tier 2 or Tier 3;
no roadmap row refuses it. That is an omission rather than a decision, which is the specific thing
this row exists to correct.

The runners-up worth a decision, all AL-flavoured rather than generic: `qualified_enum_value` (2,355,
outside the behavioural filter because of its name), swapping an enum member is the kind of change
BC code branches on constantly; `case_branch` (1,004), deleting an arm; `unary_expression` (1,124),
removing a `not`, which `negate-conditional` reaches only when the negation is a comparison.

**Next step is a decision, not a build.** Each candidate needs the same treatment the shipped ones
got: a site census, an overlap check against the mutants already emitted there, and a live
pre-committed arm in `fixtures/sandbox-data`. Shipping AOR because the literature likes it, without
measuring how many of its 1,121 sites already carry a `void-method-call` or `empty-block` mutant,
would repeat the mistake `IsolationLevelSwap` was refused for.

**Arithmetic spiked 2026-08-19.** `scripts/r159-aor-spike/` holds a prototype operator, a site
census and an `alc` compile proof; the full record is
`docs/superpowers/specs/2026-08-19-r159-aor-spike.md`. Measured on the same corpus:

- Of **1,121** arithmetic expressions, only **100** survive a compile-safety type guard. **844** are
  provably non-numeric, because **1,006 of the 1,121 tokens are `+` and AL's `+` in this app is
  mostly string concatenation**. The literature's most standard operator applies to 8.9% of the
  sites its name suggests, which is why porting it over on reputation would have been wrong.
- The guard is proven in BOTH directions: every claimed site compiles (8/8 and 3/3 on the two
  fixtures) and every refused site is rejected by `alc` with `AL0175` (4/4). A positive pass alone
  would have been satisfied by a guard that refused everything.
- **61 of the 100 are marginal** (no fine-grained mutant on the same statement): **48 additive**,
  **13 multiplicative**. R13's bar for an operator on the existing emit path is >= 13 marginal
  sites, so additive clears it by 3.7x and multiplicative lands exactly on it.

**Decision: build additive, hold multiplicative.** `x * y` to `x / y` divides by zero whenever `y`
is zero, scoring a kill that says nothing about the test, so it needs a declared
`PlatformKillMechanism` and a screen ruling first, and 13 sites does not pay for that yet.

A second measurement fell out of the spike and is filed separately as [[R160]]: the type table
cannot type a `call_expression` or a `member_expression`, so the 100 is a FLOOR and the true range
is 100 to 270.

Still undecided, and unspiked: `qualified_enum_value` (2,355), `case_branch` (1,004),
`unary_expression` (1,124). Each needs the same three measurements this one got.

**ADDITIVE BUILT AND LIVE-VERIFIED 2026-08-19.**

`lethal.swap-additive` (Tier 1, 1.0.0) flips `+` and `-` where BOTH operands resolve to a numeric
type. **93 sites on `do-rel2/Cloud`** — the spike measured 93 additive after [[R160]] lifted the
type table, up from 73 before it.

Nine mutants across two fixtures, every verdict pre-committed in
`docs/superpowers/specs/2026-08-19-r159-swap-additive-precommitment.md` and every one matching:

| gate | before | after |
| --- | --- | --- |
| `itest:tables` | 195 / 32 / 10 over 237 | **201 / 34 / 10 over 245** |
| `itest:bcdev` | 3 / 10 / 3 over 16 | **3 / 10 / 4 over 17** |

The demo campaign and `itest:hang` were measured at zero sites and did not move.

**The two survivors are the result worth reading.** `Data Main`'s `Touched` field is incremented by
two different `OnValidate` triggers and READ BY NOTHING in the suite — the identifier does not appear
in the test codeunit at all — so decrementing it instead is invisible. A genuine unasserted
behaviour inside the fixture that exists to prove operators work, found by arithmetic rather than
planted. It is the same class as the demo's `Entry Type` survivors from [[R162]].

The `itest:bcdev` mutant is `no-coverage`, not survived, and that is correct: neither of that
fixture's two tests touches `Sandbox Pricing`, so nothing executes `DiscountedPrice`. It joins the
three no-coverage mutants already there, and the gate's assertion that every no-coverage mutant lives
in `SandboxPricing.Codeunit.al` still holds.

**One gate assertion had to be widened, deliberately.** `assertFilterLiteralEvidence` pins arm I's
collateral mutant set BY OPERATOR NAME, and `swap-additive` now claims that arm's `LowBound + 3`. It
belongs there: mutated, the `SetRange` becomes `79200..79197`, an inverted range matching nothing, so
the arm's own count assertion fires. Pinning by name rather than by count is what made this a
deliberate edit with a reason instead of a silent widening.

**Multiplicative is still held**, unchanged from the spike's ruling: `x * y` to `x / y` divides by
zero whenever `y` is zero, which is a kill no test earned, and 13 marginal sites do not pay for the
`PlatformKillMechanism` and screen ruling that would need. It is kept as a separate future operator
rather than a mode of this one, because a single name would hide that the two carry entirely
different hazards.
