Found by the orchestrator, 2026-11-14, on coord task R-979 (ticket 310). Related: [[R953]] / [[R972]] (admission by memory), [[R991]].

**What happened.** `build-native-parser` under `heavy-slot.sh --wait --peak build` (1 GiB) waited 36 minutes. The held slots had declared
12 + 8 + 8 = 28 GiB, so the second check (held peaks + peak + reserve = 28 + 1 + 4 = 33 > 32) refused it on every poll, while
`memory.current` read 16.9 GiB (16.9 + 1 + 4 = 21.9 fits).

**Why.** R953's rule admits a job only when BOTH checks fit: what the container uses now, and what the held slots DECLARED (because a
young job grows to its peak later). The declared sum is the safe one, and for a large job it is right. For a job far smaller than the
gap between the declared sum and `memory.current`, it is very conservative: a 1 GiB build cannot push the container over its limit
unless the held jobs reach their full declared peaks at the same moment.

**Not decided here.** R991 (R-979) only stops a refused waiter from being overtaken forever; it does not change the admission math,
which the owner ruled (R953, R960: memory decides, both checks). A change, for example a small-job allowance (a peak at or under N GiB
needs only the current-use check) or counting a held slot at the larger of its current use and a fraction of its declared peak, would
change admission for every lane and needs the owner's ruling and a measurement of how close the held jobs come to their declared peaks.
