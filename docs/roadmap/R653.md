**Filed 2026-10-22 by lethal-code**, from R651's measured split (CDO E3a run2 at e1cd0cd8,
`/coord/handoff/R-651/live/run2/results.md`; the trace: `/coord/handoff/R-651/live/gap.md`).
Class: FULL, because it changes how a verdict is reached.

**What.** After a kill, the orchestrator confirms it: `activate(null)`, then `run(killer)`. On
serve-entries that is a live one-group request under NONE (echo + the killer's codeunit), about
200 ms each. On the CDO slice the 102 live requests took 20.6 s: 51 confirms and 51 R577
reproductions. The daemon's warm-up already ran every union codeunit under NONE, and those rows
were compared with the one-shot baseline (R635 §3), so the confirm's answer may already be held.

**Proposed step.** Serve a kill's unmutated confirm from the warm-up's NONE rows for the killer's
codeunit, with no request. The R577 reproduction stays live.

**Gain (for ranking).** 51 x about 200 ms = **about 10 s** per CDO leg: the loop goes from about
139 s to about 129 s today, or from about 78 s to about 68 s after R652.

**What the review must settle.**
1. **What does the confirm measure?** If it only asks "does the killer pass unmutated?", the
   warm-up answered that on this daemon, before any mutant ran. If it also asks "does the killer
   still pass NOW, after the mutant ran on this daemon?" (state carried over in the daemon, or a
   flaky test), the warm-up's rows answer a different question. Then reusing them removes exactly
   what the confirm exists to catch, and the step must be refused. Read the confirm's contract
   (`confirmWarm`, the cold confirm at position 1, R577) and say which it is.
2. **A daemon restart** (a cut, a dirty window) gives a new warm-up. Rows must come from the daemon
   that would have served the live request.
3. **The budget.** The confirm is judged at the budget the kill was SENT (R516/R517). A warm-up row
   ran under the daemon's stop S. Show that the two answer the same pass/fail/timeout question.
4. **Row identity.** The warm-up ran whole codeunits. Its rows for the killer must be exactly one
   row by name (R635 §6's `rowMissing` rule), never a defaulted pass.

**To close.**
- Settle question 1 first, in the plan. If the confirm measures post-mutant state, close this item
  by ruling, with the reason.
- Otherwise: one deep plan review, red-going tests for each direction (a confirm is served from
  memory only when the row is the same daemon's and the killer's own; a live request otherwise),
  and the CDO legs re-measured with per-mutant equality held as in R635.
