Found by lethal-bugs, 2026-11-12 (coord R-934, a passive memory measurement during coord R-926's serve run of Subcontracting, from
BC.History, on edge-42af5d384). Evidence: `/coord/handoff/R-934/rss.jsonl` and `rss.peaks.json` (every held heavy slot's process
tree, every 30 s) and `daemon-sub.jsonl` (each serve daemon's RSS and high-water mark against the session's scored mutants).
Related: [[R766]], [[R612]].

**What was measured.**
- The run's process tree peaked at 12.0 GB, above a BaseApp instrumentation job (about 10.6 GB, R328), while it runs as class `other`.
- One process was 9.6-9.7 GB of it: an `al-runner serve` daemon. From 10:05 to 11:00 UTC it grew from 5.8 GB to 9.7 GB (about 70 MB
  a minute), then LethAL closed it (a daemon R612 does not keep) and started a new one.
- The new daemon started at 1.9 GB and grew to 2.3 GB over two hours, while the session scored 132 more mutants (about 2.7 MB per
  scored mutant). Its RSS never fell.
- R766 measured one daemon at about 2.2 GB on D1 (a short run). Over the full 6 h of sampling the same day: Subcontracting's
  tree reached 15.7 GB (session s2) and 11.1 GB (s3), Sustainability's 12.4 GB, xl 1.1-3.0 GB, the v3 gate about 2.0 GB, and BaseApp
  v3 runs (R-938, class `baseapp`) 3.8-5.3 GB ([[R952]]).
- The container's limit is its cgroup's `memory.max`, 32 GiB (not the host's 98 GB that `free` shows; corrected 2026-11-13). So two
  long serve runs like the first daemon reach the limit, and the present caps are at risk ([[R953]]).


**Built (coord R-952).** `alRunnerV3.daemonMaxRssGb` (default 6) and `alRunnerV3.daemonMaxRequests` (default 0, off): before
a daemon is reused, `ensureDaemon` reads its VmRSS and its request count, and one past either bound is closed (close reason
`recycle`, not routine, so it reaches the close sink) and replaced by a fresh daemon, warmed up and checked against the baseline like
any restarted one. It coexists with R948's run-level cgroup stop (`memoryHeadroomGb`): the recycle keeps one daemon small, the stop
ends the run before the container's limit. The request count is of entries requests (`send()`, warm-up included); the coverage
window's plain requests and the baseline daemon's are not counted. A daemon already over the size after its warm-up and at most one
more request is the project's working set: the size bound then goes off for the session, said once
(`al-runner-v3-daemon-recycle-off`), so a large project never pays a warm-up per request. A recycle closes the lane's daemons of
both modes. The baseline's own daemon is never recycled.

**Measured verdict-neutral (2026-11-13, edge-c8fe56e05, recycle after EVERY request, `daemonMaxRequests: 1`).**
- The repro-scaffold sandbox app (default transport): 22 of 22 mutants equal in verdict and killing test (3 daemons against 2).
- Microsoft's SAF-T, one file (`--only src/AuditDataHandlingSAFT.Codeunit.al`): 67 of 67 mutants equal in verdict, killing test and
  refusal note; 57 daemons against 10; 1,365 s against 845 s.
Evidence: `/coord/handoff/R-952/` (`saft-current.*`, `saft-recycle.*`) and the scaffold's `r952-*` reports.

**The growth is real retention, filed upstream** (aldevtools/BusinessCentral.AL.Runner#7975). One daemon, the SAME `runTests` request
(SAF-T's 27 tests, cached compile, same starting database) 200 times: VmRSS 1,123 -> 1,282 MB, about 0.8 MB per request, linear.
Four other apps stay flat over 300-2,000 identical requests: the sandbox app, an invented database app, an invented XML/stream/report
app, and an invented app on the Base Application. Not reduced further. Driver and samples: `/coord/handoff/R-952/daemon-retention.ts`,
`retention-*.jsonl`.
