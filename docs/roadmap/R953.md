Filed by lethal-bugs, 2026-11-12 (coord R-934, orchestrator's ask). Related: [[R952]] (the measurements), [[R948]] (the serve daemon's
growth, the other half), [[R905]].

**What the caps assume.** `heavy-slot.sh` and `corpus-snapshot`'s guard admit at most two heavy jobs while a BaseApp-class job runs,
else three. That rule dates from a 24 GB container in which a third BaseApp corpus job ran out of memory.

**What was measured on 2026-11-12** (R952; every held slot's process tree, every 30 s, for 6 h):
- module pool build (`other`): 0.37-0.55 GB; one alc compile 0.33-0.44 GB;
- the v3 gate (`other`): about 2.0 GB;
- BaseApp v3 runs (R-938, `baseapp`): 3.8-5.3 GB; a BaseApp instrumentation job peaked at about 10.6 GB (R328);
- v3 serve runs (`other`): Subcontracting up to 15.7 GB, Sustainability up to 12.4 GB, mostly the serve daemon (R948).
So the class says little about memory: the largest jobs are long serve runs classed `other`.

**The limit is the cgroup's, not the host's.** The container's memory limit is its cgroup's `memory.max`, 32 GiB
(`/sys/fs/cgroup/memory.max` = 34,359,738,368 on 2026-11-13). `free -g` and `/proc/meminfo` show the host's 93-98 GB and are the wrong
yardstick here; this item said "the container has 93-98 GB" until 2026-11-13 (found by lethal-code in R-948's review). `memory.peak`
had reached the limit (32,772 MB), and `memory.events` recorded `oom 4`, `oom_kill 1`.

**Worst case today: already over the limit.**
- One Subcontracting serve run (15.7 GB) is about half of the 32 GiB.
- Two such runs (about 31 GB) reach the limit before the sessions, caches and other non-heavy use, which the cgroup also counts.
- Three serve runs at 12-16 GB each are about 36-48 GB: over the limit. So the present caps (three `other` jobs) can already run the
  container out of memory, and one BaseApp-class run alone reached 30.8 GB (below).

**Wanted (not built).** Admit a heavy job by memory instead of by class: a projected peak per job kind, taken from the measurements
above and refreshed by the sampler, against the cgroup's headroom (`memory.max` minus `memory.current`), with a reserve kept free.
`MemAvailable` is the host's and must not be used. The sampler (`/coord/handoff/R-934/rss-sampler.ts`) records `memory.current`,
`memory.max` and `memory.peak` every round since 2026-11-13, and each job's share of `memory.max`. R948's daemon guard (recycle a serve daemon above
a size or after N requests) is the other half: it bounds the projected peak of a serve run. The caps stay as ruled until this is built
and measured.

**Measured OOM kill, 2026-11-13** (lethal-code, coord R-942). A BaseApp-class v3 run (Continia DO Cloud with its Test app, 1,979 tests,
`alRunnerV3.license` set, master 81c59409, one heavy slot, 29 GB available at start by `free`, the host's figure) grew to a 30.8 GB peak RSS (summed over its
process tree, sampled every 30 s) while still in its baseline, 2 h 27 min in. It was SIGKILLed at 03:01:17 UTC (rc 137). The
container's cgroup recorded `oom_kill 1`, and `memory.max` was hit 536,989 times. Admission by slot count let it start, nothing bounded
its growth, and an OOM kill can take another lane's job instead. So the BaseApp class reaches 30.8 GB, not only the 3.8-10.6 GB measured
above. Since then R-942's runs carry an external watchdog: SIGTERM at 20 GB (so R955 writes the report), then SIGKILL of the run's own
tree at 26 GB. Evidence: `/coord/handoff/R-942/void-w1b/` (`do.w1.peak-rss-kb.txt` holds the peak).

**Built (coord R-952).** `scripts/heavy-slot.sh [--wait] [--peak serve|baseline|gate|build|<GiB>] <class> <cmd>`: the peak goes into
the class file (`<class> <pid> <peak>`), and the guard (`heavy-slot-guard.ts`, and `corpus-snapshot.ts`'s `guardInSlot` for its own
slots) admits a job only when BOTH fit under the cgroup's `memory.max`: used now + its peak + a 4 GiB reserve, and the held slots'
declared peaks + its peak + the reserve (`memoryVerdict` in `scripts/heavy-slot-class.ts`). Peaks: serve 16, baseline 12, gate 2,
build 1 GiB, from the measurements above and the orchestrator's interim table; an undeclared job counts at its class default (`other`
16, `baseapp` 12), an unclassed held slot at 16. With 16 GiB serve runs that admits one serve run at a time, which is what the
measurements say fits. A memory refusal is a guard refusal: exit 75, or a FIFO wait under `--wait`. The class caps are unchanged.
No cgroup limit to read (Windows, a host without cgroup v2): the rule is off and says so. Tests inject the readings
(`LETHAL_HEAVY_CGROUP_DIR`; the test preload points it at a folder that does not exist), never the real cgroup.
Not covered: the daily al-runner update decides by counting jobs, not by this rule (its gate needs about 2 GiB);
`v3-timing.ts` takes its slots without the memory rule and writes `other` at the default 16; `corpus-snapshot.ts` cannot declare a
peak, so its own slot counts at its class default (12 for a BaseApp job, 16 otherwise). A change for existing callers: an undeclared
`other` job now refuses a second undeclared one (16 + 16 + 4 > 32), so a lane script that ran two plain `heavy-slot.sh other` jobs
gets exit 75 on the second unless it declares a smaller `--peak`.
