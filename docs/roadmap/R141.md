`lethal.flip-filter-literal` refuses a filter string in two structurally different ways, and only one
of them has ever been exercised against a real server.

1. **Character refusal**, cheap and early: `REFUSED_CHARACTERS` in
   `packages/builtin-tier2/src/filter-expression.ts` is `/[*?@()'&]/`, so the mini-parser declines
   the string outright on a wildcard (`*` or `?`), a case-insensitive marker (`@`), grouping parens,
   the AND combinator (`&`), or an inner quote (the `<>''` idiom, which is the commonest `<>` shape
   in real AL). Nothing is classified and no rule ever runs.
2. **Ladder exhaustion**, late: the string CLASSIFIES successfully and then matches no rule in the
   four-rule ladder. A closed range (`'%1..%2'`) is the worked example.

R134's fixture measures the second one, at arm H, and measures it well: the site emits nothing, the
raw spec count lands on 243 rather than 244, and the gate asserts that no `flip-filter-literal`
mutant appears at that procedure. The first one is covered by one offline unit test
(`packages/builtin-tier2/tests/filter-expression.test.ts`, "refuses wildcards, at-signs,
ampersands, parens, and embedded quotes") and by nothing else. The task brief for
R134's census asked it to confirm that a `CountWild` site emits nothing; **there is no `CountWild`
anywhere in the fixture**, so there was nothing to confirm, and that absence was recorded in the
pre-commitment rather than papered over.

Why this is worth a row rather than a shrug. The two refusals fail differently when they break. A
broken ladder-exhaustion refusal splices a filter the ladder never validated, and the fixture would
notice as an aggregate mismatch. A broken CHARACTER refusal hands BC a string the parser was never
meant to reach, and the most likely outcome is a runtime filter-evaluation error, which scores
`killed` with no assertion earning it and nothing tagging the mechanism (R86, R138). That is the
false-kill shape this project exists to catch, and on the `<>''` population it would be the single
most common one, because `<>''` is where rule 1's real reach mostly goes.

What would close this: one fixture arm whose `SetFilter` carries a refused character class (`<>''`
is the highest-value choice, since it is both the commonest idiom and the one whose accidental
mutation is most likely to raise inside BC), with the emitted-nothing prediction pre-committed and
the raw spec count asserted, exactly as arm H does for ladder exhaustion. Cost is one procedure, one
test and one baseline re-record.
