**Tier-3 operators.** **CLOSED 2026-08-02 — designed, measured, refused on COST, and the category itself dissolved.** Decision: `docs/superpowers/specs/2026-08-02-r13-tier3-decision.md`; measurements: `docs/measurements/README.md` §R13; probe: `scripts/r13-probe/`. Thresholds were pre-committed at `349901a` BEFORE any candidate was counted (calibrated on the shipped set on `do-rel2/Cloud`, 19,132 mutants, smallest shipped operator `swap-modify-flag` = 13): (a) fits the existing guarded emit path -> >=13 mutants; (b) needs a new activation mechanism -> >=5% (957); (c) measured unkillable -> never. **The premise did not survive.** `design.md` called all three operators "object metadata" needing "a distinct emit path"; measured against the grammar, only ONE is declarative. `IsolationLevelSwap` targets `call_expression`/`assignment_statement` in statement position — the SAME path Tier 2 uses — and 25 of its 36 sites ALREADY carry a shipped `lethal.void-method-call` mutant, so its marginal footprint is **11**, below bar (a): refused on the cost bar alone. `EventPublisherSignature`’s signature form is emittable only as a duplicate publisher that no subscriber binds to (equivalent by construction); the observable version is an argument swap at the RAISE site, which is a different, non-event-specific operator — re-filed as **R82**. `PermissionReduce` is the only true metadata operator: 38 `Permissions` properties / **423** `tabledata` grants (2.21%) against bar (b)’s 957 — refused on cost. **It is NOT refused as worthless: it is KILLABLE, measured.** The first draft of the decision claimed "unkillable in both of the two modes a suite can be in"; an adversarial review found a third mode in the censused project itself (a test declaring `TestPermissions = Disabled` that then calls `LibraryLowerPermissions.SetO365Basic()`), and arm A8 measured that reducing `rm`->`r` on a callee turns a succeeding write into `Sorry, the current permissions prevented the action.` — with a direct-write control proving the lowering took effect. Reachability bounds the value: **10 of 1,290 tests (0.78%), in 2 of 104 test files**. Reopen if a project shows >=957 grant sites or permission-lowering stops being <1% of a suite.

## AMENDED 2026-08-31, CORRECTED TWICE THE SAME DAY: grain, grounds, floor, and the corpus it is relative to

Bar (a) said ">= 13 marginal sites" and never said at what grain a site counts as already covered.
[[R181]] measured that the answer swings from 88.4% to 23.7% marginal across three defensible grains
on the same 1,998 mutants and the same operators, so the bar was **underdetermined from the start**
and its observed tightening was a symptom. Applied today it refuses `swap-additive`, which shipped.

**This replaces bar (a) only. Bars (b) and (c) are untouched** — R181 examined neither.

### 1. Grain: the canonical minimal edit

Two mutants are the same edit when, after trimming the common prefix and suffix of the original text
against the replacement, they rewrite the same bytes to the same bytes.

NOT `dedup.ts`'s raw identity, and the difference has a direction. Dedup wants distinct mutants kept
distinct, so over-splitting is its SAFE error; an admission bar prices redundancy, so over-splitting
is its UNSAFE error. Under the raw identity an operator that frames a wider span around the same
rewrite mints a "new" edit producing a byte-identical program. Measured instance: `shift-integer`
rewrites the literal in `Arr[5]` and a subscript operator rewrites `[5]`; the raw identity calls
those different, and R159's own recorded figure for `subscript_expression` (53, not 143) already
embodied the reduction without stating it.

Adopting it costs the retrodiction nothing: on `do-rel2/Cloud` both grains give identical
per-operator counts and the same 972 collisions, because no two SHIPPED operators frame different
spans around one rewrite. It changes CANDIDATE pricing, which is where the defect lived.
`scripts/r181-effect-grain-retrodiction.ts --canonical` measures it; the default keeps the raw
identity so the original evidence stays reproducible.

Still textual, not semantic. Two edits producing different bytes but equivalent programs do not
collide, and nothing here can see that they are equivalent.

### 2. Grounds, any one of which admits

1. **Coverage** — edits nothing shipped emits. Priced by the floor below.
2. **Attribution** — an edit that already exists, under its own name. Admitted only when the
   partition follows a **platform-semantic class**: a named BC operation with its own failure
   semantics, which is what `SetRange`, `CalcFields`, `TestField` and `Commit` are. Without that
   gate the ground admits any renaming of an arbitrary subset. The justification is measured, not
   aesthetic — `--operator` scoping ([[R127]]) made R183's and R172's questions answerable in minutes,
   `operatorName` is one of [[R166]]'s five identity fields, and every census groups by it. It is
   deliberately NOT justified by developer recognition, which is plausible and unmeasurable.
   Admission on this ground CHANGES gate identities and is paid deliberately.
3. **Hazard removal** — removes a hazard rather than adding sites, as [[R179]] ruled for `loop-skip`.

### 3. Floor, ground 1 only, and the corpus it is relative to

**>= 36 marginal SITES on the reference corpus `do-rel2/Cloud`, content sha256
`9a8e8831449208cc48bdbc8f04ae1db42d6050ca67bd7487350f48cc6b933962` (417 `.al` files, excluding
`.dependencies`).** The hash is the identity, not the path: `do-lethal/Cloud` is the same corpus
under another path ([[R187]]), and `do-rel2` is a worktree that can move. A candidate priced on a
corpus with a different hash is not priced against this floor.
`scripts/corpus-fingerprint.ts` computes it and `scripts/r181-effect-grain-retrodiction.ts` prints
it as its first line.

- **Sites, not edits.** The first version said edits, which a candidate inflates by emitting several
  variants per span: `time_literal` at 12 sites clears any floor once its operator emits three shift
  variants each. Shipped operators run at about one edit per span, so the floor was calibrated in
  sites and would have been enforced in edits. Count distinct spans.
- **Calibrated against the smallest shipped operator admitted on GROUND 1**, which is
  `swap-rec-xrec` at 36. Operators admitted on another ground are excluded, because they are in the
  set precisely for not clearing a coverage bar. That excludes the four ground-2 operators and also
  `loop-skip` at 23, which [[R179]] admitted on hazard while recording that its count was "well
  under R13's bar of 13".
- **The floor is a property of that corpus, not of the rule, and this is now stated rather than
  implied.** Measured: on `DC/Cloud` (Continia Document Capture, 475 files, 29,940 edits)
  `swap-enum-member` emits **0**, because that product predates enums, and the smallest non-zero
  ground-1 operator is `swap-find-direction` at **44**. On `BusinessCentral.Sentinel` (different
  vendor, 67 files) 14 of 25 shipped operators fall under even the old floor of 13. A candidate is
  priced on the reference corpus; a number from any other is not comparable to this floor.
- **A candidate clearing by a thin margin is UNDECIDED, not admitted.** The floor moves 36 to 44
  across the two production corpora measured, so anything inside that band is noise. Clearing needs
  daylight, or the candidate needs evidence of a different kind.

### 4. What this does NOT do

**It does not re-admit `IsolationLevelSwap`, and that is a decision rather than an omission.** Its
edits are swaps and never deletions, so its effect-grain collisions are 0 and its marginal is 36 —
which ties the floor exactly. Ruled: a tie on a corpus-relative floor is the thin-margin case above,
so the grain change makes it RECONSIDERABLE and does not admit it. Reconsideration owes discrimination
evidence (does it separate suites the shipped set scores identically), not a re-run of the count.
R13's original refusal stands until then.

### 5. Known limits, recorded rather than discovered later

- The calibrator was never judged by any bar. `swap-rec-xrec` shipped `ff08e8c` on 2026-08-02, the
  day the bar was written. "Smallest shipped operator on ground 1" means "smallest that happened to
  ship early and retroactively classifies as ground 1". Inherited from this row's own method.
- Ground 1 now prices only raw yield plus exact duplication. **Same-site different-edit redundancy,
  which is what bar (a) was created to price, is priced by nothing.** That axis is [[R181]]'s
  discrimination measurement, which exists and does not yet have enough shared sites to rank by.

### Why this rule and not a tidier one

It was **retrodicted before adoption**, and twice corrected by that discipline rather than by
argument. 21 of 25 shipped operators clear on ground 1; the four that fail do so at 100% against
`void-method-call` because they emit the same empty replacement on the same nodes — 673 + 174 + 78 +
47 = 972, exactly its collision count. That is not four bad operators, it is the ground the old bar
had no slot for.

Evidence: `scripts/r181-effect-grain-retrodiction.ts` (with and without `--canonical`),
`scripts/r181-discrimination-census.ts`, and [[R181]]'s measured sections.
