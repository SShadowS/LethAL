**Tier-3 operators.** **CLOSED 2026-08-02 — designed, measured, refused on COST, and the category itself dissolved.** Decision: `docs/superpowers/specs/2026-08-02-r13-tier3-decision.md`; measurements: `docs/measurements/README.md` §R13; probe: `scripts/r13-probe/`. Thresholds were pre-committed at `349901a` BEFORE any candidate was counted (calibrated on the shipped set on `do-rel2/Cloud`, 19,132 mutants, smallest shipped operator `swap-modify-flag` = 13): (a) fits the existing guarded emit path -> >=13 mutants; (b) needs a new activation mechanism -> >=5% (957); (c) measured unkillable -> never. **The premise did not survive.** `design.md` called all three operators "object metadata" needing "a distinct emit path"; measured against the grammar, only ONE is declarative. `IsolationLevelSwap` targets `call_expression`/`assignment_statement` in statement position — the SAME path Tier 2 uses — and 25 of its 36 sites ALREADY carry a shipped `lethal.void-method-call` mutant, so its marginal footprint is **11**, below bar (a): refused on the cost bar alone. `EventPublisherSignature`’s signature form is emittable only as a duplicate publisher that no subscriber binds to (equivalent by construction); the observable version is an argument swap at the RAISE site, which is a different, non-event-specific operator — re-filed as **R82**. `PermissionReduce` is the only true metadata operator: 38 `Permissions` properties / **423** `tabledata` grants (2.21%) against bar (b)’s 957 — refused on cost. **It is NOT refused as worthless: it is KILLABLE, measured.** The first draft of the decision claimed "unkillable in both of the two modes a suite can be in"; an adversarial review found a third mode in the censused project itself (a test declaring `TestPermissions = Disabled` that then calls `LibraryLowerPermissions.SetO365Basic()`), and arm A8 measured that reducing `rm`->`r` on a callee turns a succeeding write into `Sorry, the current permissions prevented the action.` — with a direct-write control proving the lowering took effect. Reachability bounds the value: **10 of 1,290 tests (0.78%), in 2 of 104 test files**. Reopen if a project shows >=957 grant sites or permission-lowering stops being <1% of a suite.

## AMENDED 2026-08-31: the admission rule names a GRAIN, three GROUNDS, and a floor of 23

Bar (a) above said ">= 13 marginal sites" and never said at what grain a site is already covered.
[[R181]] measured that the answer swings from 88.4% marginal to 23.7% across three defensible grains
on the same 1,998 mutants and the same operators, so the bar was **underdetermined from the start**;
its observed tightening was a symptom. It also measured that bar (a) applied today refuses
`swap-additive`, which this project shipped and values.

**This amendment replaces bar (a) only. Bars (b) and (c) are untouched**: a candidate needing a new
activation mechanism still owes >= 5%, and one measured unkillable is still refused outright. Nothing
in R181 examined either.

### Grain

**Effect grain**: two mutants are the same when `dedup.ts`'s identity is the same, i.e.
`kind:startIndex:endIndex:after.text`. Same span AND same replacement text. This is the identity the
compiler already enforces, and the grain [[R82]] already reasoned at.

### Grounds, any one of which admits an operator

1. **Coverage** — it emits edits nothing shipped emits. Priced by the floor below.
2. **Attribution** — it emits an edit that already exists, but under its own name. Measured, not
   asserted: `--operator` scoping ([[R127]]) makes a question answerable in minutes that is otherwise
   unaskable; `operatorName` is one of [[R166]]'s five identity fields and these operators hold 20
   mutants in the frozen `tables.baseline.json`; and every census in this project groups by operator
   name. Deliberately NOT justified by developer recognition, which is plausible and unmeasurable.
3. **Hazard removal** — it removes a hazard rather than adding sites. Already ruled this way by
   [[R179]] when it accepted `loop-skip`.

### Floor, ground 1 only

**>= 36 marginal edits at effect grain**, calibrated exactly as this row calibrated 13: against the
smallest shipped operator **admitted on ground 1**. At effect grain on `do-rel2/Cloud` (417 files,
29,078 distinct edits) that is `swap-rec-xrec` at 36.

Operators admitted on another ground are excluded from a ground-1 calibration, because including
them is circular: they are in the set precisely because they did NOT clear a coverage bar. That
excludes the four ground-2 operators, whose zeros are the point, and it also excludes `loop-skip` at
23 — [[R179]] admitted it on hazard, recording in terms that its marginal count was "well under R13's
bar of 13" and that "the bar was written for coverage, not for hazard".

**CORRECTED 2026-08-31, hours after this amendment first landed at 23.** The first version excluded
the ground-2 operators as circular and then calibrated against a ground-3 one, which is the same
circularity it had just named. The error mattered: at 23 `parenthesized_expression` (32) cleared,
and at 36 it refuses.

### Why this rule and not a tidier one

It was **retrodicted before being adopted**, which is the test a rule that only accepts new proposals
never faces. 21 of 25 shipped operators clear on ground 1. The four that do not fail at 100%, and the
arithmetic names the counterparty without looking: 673 + 174 + 78 + 47 = 972, exactly
`void-method-call`'s collision count. `remove-setrange`, `remove-calcfields`, `remove-testfield` and
`remove-commit` all emit `synthesizeAfter(node, "")` on the same nodes it does. That is not four bad
operators; it is a ground the old bar had no slot for, and finding it is why the retrodiction was run.

Evidence: `scripts/r181-effect-grain-retrodiction.ts`, `scripts/r181-discrimination-census.ts`, and
[[R181]]'s measured sections.
