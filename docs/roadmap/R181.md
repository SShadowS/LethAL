## What breaks, and for whom

R13 set the bar for a new operator on the existing emit path at **>= 13 marginal sites**, where
marginal means "no fine-grained mutant already on the same statement". It was calibrated on the
shipped set as of 2026-08-02, against the smallest shipped operator (`swap-modify-flag` = 13).

The bar measures a candidate against **whatever is already shipped**. So every operator that lands
makes the next candidate look more redundant, and the bar tightens itself with no one deciding that
it should. Measured on `do-rel2/Cloud`:

| operator set | `swap-additive`'s marginal sites |
| --- | ---: |
| as of 2026-08-19, when its spike ran | **77** (the spike published 61; the gap is R160's lifted type guard and the 4.0.0 grammar bump since) |
| as of 2026-08-28, today | **3** |

**`swap-additive` was accepted at 3.7x the bar and would now be refused at a quarter of it.** Nothing
about the operator changed. Nine operators shipped in nine days ([[R159]]'s wave: `remove-assignment`,
`toggle-blank-string`, `flip-boolean-literal`, `shift-integer`, `remove-not`, `negate-guard`,
`swap-enum-member`, `loop-truncate`, `loop-skip`), and each one claims statements that make the next
candidate's sites look already-covered.

## Why this surfaced now

[[R159]]'s remaining candidates were sized 2026-08-28 and **every one refuses** on this test:

| candidate | sites | marginal today | |
| --- | ---: | ---: | --- |
| `parenthesized_expression` (boolean regrouping) | 32 | **0** | all 32 already carry a `negate-conditional` mutant on the inner expression |
| temporal literals (`date`/`datetime`/`time`) | 125 | **7** | |
| `subscript_expression` (non-literal index) | 53 | **2** | |
| `foreach_statement` | 13 | **0** | |
| `for_statement` | 46 | 0 claimable | refuses for a different and sound reason: `shift-integer` already claims every one |

A clean sweep of refusals is exactly the result that should be distrusted, and checking it against a
shipped operator is what showed the sweep is the method talking rather than the candidates.

## What is NOT being claimed

- **Not that the bar is wrong to exist.** It correctly refused `IsolationLevelSwap` (25 of 36 sites
  already carried a `void-method-call` mutant) and `PermissionReduce` on cost. Diminishing returns
  are real and a bar that notices them is doing its job.
- **Not that these candidates should now be built.** Their marginal counts really are low, and
  "the bar is miscalibrated" is not evidence that any particular one earns its cost.
- **Not that `for_statement` should be revisited.** Its refusal does not depend on this at all: it
  has zero claimable sites because `shift-integer` claims the literal bound at every one of the 46.

## The actual question, which is a decision nobody has taken

**What is the right marginality test once the operator set is mature?** Three shapes, none chosen:

1. **Statement granularity is too coarse.** `if (A + B) > C then Foo();` has an `empty-block`, a
   `conditional-boundary` and a `void-method-call` mutant in it, so ANY new operator targeting
   anything in that line is "non-marginal" — even though it asks a question none of those three ask.
   A per-EXPRESSION or per-fault-class test would say something different.
2. **The bar should be relative, not absolute.** ">= 13 sites" was a count on one corpus at one
   moment. As a fraction of the shipped mutant total it has silently moved.
3. **Marginality may be the wrong axis entirely.** What a reader wants is DISCRIMINATION — does this
   operator separate suites that the existing ones score identically? [[R121]]'s assertion screen and
   [[R134]]'s disagreeing verdicts are the shape of a measurement that answers that, and neither is a
   site count. `flip-boolean-literal` is the precedent: [[R159]] recorded that `empty-block` KILLS
   procedures where the fine-grained flip SURVIVES, so coarse and fine disagree at the same sites.
   Under a statement-granularity test those sites are "already covered"; under a discrimination test
   they are the whole point.

Option 3 is the one that matches how this project already argues about operators, and it is the most
work: it needs a measurement instrument, not a rule change.

## Evidence

`scripts/r159-remainder-census.ts` sizes the candidates. The marginality figures and the
`swap-additive` control were measured with a throwaway probe over `[...tier1Operators,
...tier2Operators]`, excluding the operator under test and, for the historical column, everything
R159 shipped after 2026-08-19. Reproducing it needs no new tooling: the control is the load-bearing
part, and it is one operator's `targets()` measured against two operator sets.
