## What breaks, and for whom

R13 set the bar for a new operator on the existing emit path at **>= 13 marginal sites**, where
marginal means "no fine-grained mutant already on the same statement". It was calibrated on the
shipped set as of 2026-08-02, against the smallest shipped operator (`swap-modify-flag` = 13).

The bar measures a candidate against **whatever is already shipped**. So every operator that lands
makes the next candidate look more redundant, and the bar tightens itself with no one deciding that
it should. Measured on `do-rel2/Cloud`:

| operator set | `swap-additive`'s marginal sites |
| --- | ---: |
| as of 2026-08-19, when its spike ran | **77** (the spike published 61; the gap is R160's lifted type guard and the 4.0.0 grammar bump since) |
| as of 2026-08-28, today | **3** |

**`swap-additive` was accepted at 3.7x the bar and would now be refused at a quarter of it.** Nothing
about the operator changed. Nine operators shipped in nine days ([[R159]]'s wave: `remove-assignment`,
`toggle-blank-string`, `flip-boolean-literal`, `shift-integer`, `remove-not`, `negate-guard`,
`swap-enum-member`, `loop-truncate`, `loop-skip`), and each one claims statements that make the next
candidate's sites look already-covered.

## Why this surfaced now

[[R159]]'s remaining candidates were sized 2026-08-28 and **every one refuses** on this test:

| candidate | sites | marginal today | |
| --- | ---: | ---: | --- |
| `parenthesized_expression` (boolean regrouping) | 32 | **0** | all 32 already carry a `negate-conditional` mutant on the inner expression |
| temporal literals (`date`/`datetime`/`time`) | 125 | **7** | |
| `subscript_expression` (non-literal index) | 53 | **2** | |
| `foreach_statement` | 13 | **0** | |
| `for_statement` | 46 | 0 claimable | refuses for a different and sound reason: `shift-integer` already claims every one |

A clean sweep of refusals is exactly the result that should be distrusted, and checking it against a
shipped operator is what showed the sweep is the method talking rather than the candidates.

## What is NOT being claimed

- **Not that the bar is wrong to exist.** It correctly refused `IsolationLevelSwap` (25 of 36 sites
  already carried a `void-method-call` mutant) and `PermissionReduce` on cost. Diminishing returns
  are real and a bar that notices them is doing its job.
- **Not that these candidates should now be built.** Their marginal counts really are low, and
  "the bar is miscalibrated" is not evidence that any particular one earns its cost.
- **Not that `for_statement` should be revisited.** Its refusal does not depend on this at all: it
  has zero claimable sites because `shift-integer` claims the literal bound at every one of the 46.

## The actual question, which is a decision nobody has taken

**What is the right marginality test once the operator set is mature?** Three shapes, none chosen:

1. **Statement granularity is too coarse.** `if (A + B) > C then Foo();` has an `empty-block`, a
   `conditional-boundary` and a `void-method-call` mutant in it, so ANY new operator targeting
   anything in that line is "non-marginal" — even though it asks a question none of those three ask.
   A per-EXPRESSION or per-fault-class test would say something different.
2. **The bar should be relative, not absolute.** ">= 13 sites" was a count on one corpus at one
   moment. As a fraction of the shipped mutant total it has silently moved.
3. **Marginality may be the wrong axis entirely.** What a reader wants is DISCRIMINATION — does this
   operator separate suites that the existing ones score identically? [[R121]]'s assertion screen and
   [[R134]]'s disagreeing verdicts are the shape of a measurement that answers that, and neither is a
   site count. `flip-boolean-literal` is the precedent: [[R159]] recorded that `empty-block` KILLS
   procedures where the fine-grained flip SURVIVES, so coarse and fine disagree at the same sites.
   Under a statement-granularity test those sites are "already covered"; under a discrimination test
   they are the whole point.

Option 3 is the one that matches how this project already argues about operators, and it is the most
work: it needs a measurement instrument, not a rule change.

## Evidence

`scripts/r159-remainder-census.ts` sizes the candidates. The marginality figures and the
`swap-additive` control were measured with a throwaway probe over `[...tier1Operators,
...tier2Operators]`, excluding the operator under test and, for the historical column, everything
R159 shipped after 2026-08-19. Reproducing it needs no new tooling: the control is the load-bearing
part, and it is one operator's `targets()` measured against two operator sets.

## MEASURED 2026-08-29: the grain decides the answer, and the bar never fixed one

`scripts/r181-discrimination-census.ts` runs over 1,998 live-measured mutants from six committed
campaign reports (one per distinct mutant SET, so the three repeat measurements of `do rung1` do not
treble-count it). Nothing about the operators changes between these rows. Only the question does:

| grain | groups | solo mutants | marginal |
| --- | ---: | ---: | ---: |
| line (`file:line`) | 1,734 | 1,766 | **88.4%** |
| procedure | 492 | 510 | 25.5% |
| file | 116 | 473 | 23.7% |

**R13's bar fixes a threshold (>=13 marginal sites) and never fixes a grain.** So "is this operator
marginal" has no answer until someone picks one, and the answer swings from overwhelmingly yes to
overwhelmingly no across choices that are all defensible.

That is a better diagnosis than this row's original one. The bar does not merely tighten as operators
ship; **it was underdetermined from the start**, and the tightening is one symptom.

### What it does to [[R159]]'s refused candidates

R159's remainder was refused at STATEMENT grain, where marginal came out ~0 for every candidate. The
same candidates were also measured at exact-span grain, which is essentially site grain, and their
overlap with every shipped operator is **0** — so at site grain their marginal counts are their full
counts:

| candidate | marginal at statement grain | at site grain | vs the bar of 13 |
| --- | ---: | ---: | --- |
| `parenthesized_expression` | 0 | 32 | clears |
| `subscript_expression` | 2 | 53 | clears |
| `date_literal` | 6 | 69 | clears |
| `datetime_literal` | 0 | 44 | clears |
| `time_literal` | 1 | 12 | below alone, 125 as one temporal operator |
| `foreach_statement` | 0 | 13 | exactly at it |

**Five of six flip from refused to accepted on the grain choice alone.** R159's deferral was right to
be a deferral.

### Discrimination is measurable, and this corpus cannot yet rank by it

The script also measures the axis this row proposed: where two operators put a mutant on the SAME
site, do their kill/survive verdicts DISAGREE? Disagreement is resolution — the operator is telling a
reader something the other one does not. It is the same evidence shape `itest:tables` already pins
for `flip-filter-literal`, where a flip and a deletion disagree in opposite directions at two sites.

It works, and it is not yet usable for a decision: **only 95 of 1,808 sites are shared by two or more
operators**, so most operators have single-digit pair counts and the newest ten barely appear in
reports that predate them. `swap-enum-member` reads 100% on three pairs. That is not a ranking.

What the corpus does show is worth keeping: `empty-block`, the coarsest operator, has the LOWEST
disagreement rate of any operator with meaningful data (2 of 15 pairs, 13.3%), while
`void-method-call` is 9 of 14 (64.3%). A coarse operator agreeing with everything is exactly the
prediction, and it is the first direct evidence for it.

## The decision this now needs, narrowed to one question

Not "is the bar too tight" but **"at what grain is a mutant redundant?"** Three answers, and the
measurement above prices each:

1. **Site (`file:line`).** The finest grain actually recorded, and the only version of "something
   else already mutates this" that is not an artifact of grouping. Accepts most candidates, so the
   threshold of 13 would need recalibrating upward or it stops binding at all.
2. **Procedure.** What a reader navigates by. Keeps the bar biting (25.5% marginal) but calls two
   operators redundant for sharing a 40-line procedure, which is not what redundancy means.
3. **Keep statement grain and lower the threshold.** Preserves today's refusals and leaves the
   underdetermination in place for the next candidate.

Whichever is chosen must be WRITTEN DOWN on R13, because the current bar's number has outlived the
unstated assumption it was calibrated against.

## RETRODICTED 2026-08-29, and it found a third thing the bar conflates

The proposal was to judge redundancy at EFFECT grain: a mutant is redundant only where a shipped
operator already produces the same edit, which is the identity `packages/schemata/src/dedup.ts`
already enforces (`kind:startIndex:endIndex:after.text`) and the grain [[R82]] reasoned at in its own
words, contrasting a swap's replacement with `void-method-call`'s deletion "unlike
`IsolationLevelSwap`, whose 25-of-36 overlap was a genuine identity collision".

**A rule that accepts a new proposal proves nothing. The test is whether it accepts what is already
built.** `scripts/r181-effect-grain-retrodiction.ts` measures every shipped operator against the set
minus itself, at effect grain, over `do-rel2/Cloud` (417 files, 29,078 distinct edits).

**Twenty-one of twenty-five clear. Four do not, and all four fail at 100%:**

| operator | emitted | collided | marginal |
| --- | ---: | ---: | ---: |
| `lethal.remove-setrange` | 673 | 673 | **0** |
| `lethal.remove-calcfields` | 174 | 174 | **0** |
| `lethal.remove-testfield` | 78 | 78 | **0** |
| `lethal.remove-commit` | 47 | 47 | **0** |

The arithmetic identifies the counterparty without needing to look: 673 + 174 + 78 + 47 = **972**,
which is exactly `void-method-call`'s own collision count. Every collision in the corpus is one of
these four against it. The mechanism is mechanical rather than coincidental: both sides emit
`synthesizeAfter(node, "")`, the empty replacement, on the same node. Identical span, identical
replacement text, identical dedup identity.

### That is not a refutation of the rule. It is a third conflation the bar was hiding

These four operators emit **no edit `void-method-call` would not already emit**. §3.2 precedence
gives Tier 2 the win, so the mutant survives dedup carrying the specific name — `remove-setrange`
rather than `void-method-call`. The mutant exists either way. What the Tier-2 operator adds is the
EXPLANATION.

So the bar was conflating three things, not two:

1. **New edits** — coverage. What effect-grain marginality measures.
2. **Better naming of edits that already exist** — explanation. What these four do, and what no bar
   in this project has ever had a slot for.
3. **Hazard removal** — safety. Already ruled on separately by [[R179]], which accepted `loop-skip`
   on hazards removed rather than sites added.

An admission rule with only ground 1 refuses four operators the project shipped and values. That is
the rule being incomplete, not the operators being wrong — and it is exactly what the retrodiction
existed to catch, because neither the proposal nor its author predicted it.

### What the retrodiction confirmed as predicted

`swap-additive`, the operator whose refusal by the current bar started this row, comes back **51
emitted / 0 collided / 51 marginal — clears**. Under effect grain the operator that today's bar
would refuse at 3-against-13 passes with no collision at all.

### Where this leaves the decision

Unchanged in shape, sharper in content. The ruling still belongs on R13 and still has to name a
redundancy test, but it now has to name **three admission grounds** rather than one, and record that
an operator qualifying only on ground 2 adds explanation and not coverage — which is a real thing to
buy, and a different thing from what a site count prices.

Not measured, and it is the obvious next question: whether ground 2 is worth its cost, i.e. whether a
reader given `remove-setrange` acts differently from one given `void-method-call` at the same line.
That is a question about readers, not corpora, and no instrument here answers it.

### Ground 2 is ATTRIBUTION, not presentation, and it is measurable

The obvious objection to ground 2 is that it is cosmetic: `remove-setrange` and `void-method-call`
produce a byte-identical mutant, so an agent reading a survivor list learns nothing from the name.
**True for reading a verdict, and false for three other things**, two of which this row's own
investigation depended on:

1. **Scoping a run.** [[R127]]'s `--operator lethal.remove-setrange` narrows a run to exactly those
   mutants. That is what made R183's and R172's questions answerable in minutes: `--operator
   lethal.remove-commit` ran 3 mutants where the unnarrowed fixture is 345 and would have taken
   about five hours on the al-runner backend. With only `void-method-call` there is no way to ask
   for the SetRange deletions; the request returns all 8,269 void-call deletions on the corpus.
2. **Gate identity.** `operatorName` is one of the five fields in [[R166]]'s identity key. These four
   operators hold **20 mutants in the frozen `tables.baseline.json`** — 12 `remove-setrange`, 4
   `remove-testfield`, 3 `remove-commit`, 1 `remove-calcfields` — so collapsing them into
   `void-method-call` changes 20 identities and the per-mutant gate fails. Self-referential, since
   the key includes the name by our own choice, but a real cost that has to be paid deliberately.
3. **The project's own instruments.** Every census in this row groups by operator name. Collapsing
   the four costs exactly the resolution the discrimination census is supposed to provide.

So the ground is better named **attribution** than "naming" or "explanation": the tool's job is to
say what changed and why the suite missed it, and "the `SetRange` was removed" answers that more
precisely than "a void call was deleted". Framed that way it needs no appeal to taste.

**Deliberately NOT part of the justification: that mutation testing is new in AL and a developer may
recognise `remove-setrange` more readily.** That is plausible and probably true, and it is
unmeasurable without a study of AL developers nobody is going to run. Ground 2 does not need it —
scoping alone carries the decision — and resting a rule on an unmeasured comprehension claim is what
this project refuses everywhere else.

**Nothing here proposes deleting those four operators.** The open question is only what R13's rule
says; the retrodiction settled that it needs a ground-2 slot or it refuses operators already shipped.
