**Filed by R-735** (2026-10-24). [[R735]] compiles each suspect alc names ALONE before anything else, and there is
no cap on how many suspects one round may try. One culprit whose error spreads over many later branches (a cascade)
makes alc point at many innocent mutants. Each costs one singleton compile. The budget (`MAX_ISOLATION_COMPILES =
64`) can run out on innocents, and everything is left unresolved, where plain halving would have isolated the
culprit in about log2(n) + 2 compiles.

**It fails closed.** Unresolved mutants stay `error` with the not-attributable note. No wrong verdict is possible,
but the verdicts are lost.

**Measured.** DC showed no cascade: 30 injected culprits isolated in 34 compiles, 2 in 6 (see [[R735]]). The risk is
unmeasured on a project whose errors do spread.

**Fix idea.** A small per-round cap on suspects (for example 8). When a round has more, try the cap's worth, then
fall back to halving for that round.

**Done 2026-10-25 by R-743 (lethal-preproc).**
- **Built (`bisect.ts`):** `MAX_CLEARED_SUSPECTS_PER_ROUND = 8`. In one round, at most 8 suspects may compile fine
  ALONE (be cleared as innocents). After the 8th, that round stops trying suspects and halves. A suspect that FAILS
  alone (a culprit) does not count toward the cap.
- **Why innocents only, not every suspect:** R735's measured DC runs cleared no innocents. In `--isolate 30`
  (`/coord/handoff/R-735/dc/isolate-30.compiles.log`), the full set's round tried 15 suspects alone and all 15 were
  culprits; the recompile's round did the same. A cap counting every suspect would have split each round into rounds
  of 8 and added recompiles (34 → 36). Counting innocents only leaves R735's figures unchanged:
  - DC: 34 for 30 culprits, 6 for 2;
  - the live re-run: 5;
  - the unit test B4: 33.
  No compile count moves, so nothing needed a pre-commitment, and no re-run was made.
- **Tests:** `bisect-suspects.test.ts` B11-B14, test-first.
  - B11 is the cascade: one culprit among 2000, alc pointing at 60 innocents. It was red before the fix, with 62
    singleton compiles, then the early stop and everything unresolved. Now 8 innocents are tried, then halving.
  - B12: 20 culprits named at once are all tried in one round (23 compiles).
  - B13 and B14 pin the boundary: 7 innocents, then the culprit, is still a suspect; 8 innocents, then the culprit,
    halves.
- **Red-checked per direction:**
  - no cap: B11 and B14 red;
  - culprits counted too: B12 red, and R735's B4 too;
  - a cap of 7: B13 red;
  - a cap of 9: B14 red.
- **Limits:** the cap is a fixed 8, not measured on a project whose errors really cascade. A cascade still costs up to
  8 compiles per round.
