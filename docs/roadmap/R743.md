**Filed by R-735** (2026-10-24). [[R735]] compiles each suspect alc names ALONE before anything else, and there is
no cap on how many suspects one round may try. One culprit whose error spreads over many later branches (a cascade)
makes alc point at many innocent mutants. Each costs one singleton compile. The budget (`MAX_ISOLATION_COMPILES =
64`) can run out on innocents, and everything is left unresolved, where plain halving would have isolated the
culprit in about log2(n) + 2 compiles.

**It fails closed.** Unresolved mutants stay `error` with the not-attributable note. No wrong verdict is possible,
but the verdicts are lost.

**Measured.** DC showed no cascade: 30 injected culprits isolated in 34 compiles, 2 in 6 (see [[R735]]). The risk is
unmeasured on a project whose errors do spread.

**Fix idea.** A small per-round cap on suspects (for example 8). When a round has more, try the cap's worth, then
fall back to halving for that round.
