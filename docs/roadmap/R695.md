**Filed 2026-10-23 by lethal-preproc during R-604b** ([[R604]]), from S3's side note (`/coord/handoff/R-604b/measure.md`,
"## S3: all 8 sites").

**What.** In BC.History BaseApp, the report `Fixed Asset - Projected Value`, procedure `CalculateFirstDeprAmount`,
loops until its until-date reaches the ending date (or `Done`). The until-date is advanced by the report's own
`GetNextDate`.
- A mutant in `GetNextDate` that stops advancing that date could keep the loop from ending.
- A key-name search of the refused set finds no refused procedure in that report, on master or on the R-604b head
  (`fpvHead = []` and `fpvMaster = []` in `/coord/handoff/R-604b/head-bch-one.jsonl`; keys are lower-cased object
  and procedure names, so the search would have matched).

**Why it matters.** Such a mutant would hang. Design §6.7 and [[R164]] then stop it and score it `timeout-killed`, a
real kill. So this costs time, not correctness: no false kill.

**Not yet verified.** Nobody has read the loop by parse to confirm that `GetNextDate`'s write is the exit's only
advance, and that no rule refuses it under another name.

**To close.**
- Read the loop by parse: the until-date's writers, and whether R572's state rules should see `GetNextDate` as a
  loop-exit writer.
- Then refuse it, with a red-checked test, or close by ruling with the reason.

**Closed by ruling (R-573, 2026-10-23); the premise above is corrected.** Evidence: `/coord/handoff/R-573/measure.md`.
The procedure IS refused today. R487's `inOpenItemCode` scope (`GetNextDate` is reached from the report's
`OnAfterGetRecord`) refuses 161 of 162 nodes of `GetNextDate` and 131 of 132 nodes of `CalculateFirstDeprAmount`,
for all 10 operators tried. The one node left in each is the `procedure` node itself. The dump agrees: no deployed
key in `GetNextDate`, `CalculateFirstDeprAmount` or `CalculateSecondDeprAmount`.
The hang risk is real and covered: by parse, `UntilDate := UntilDate2` in `GetNextDate` is the loop exit's only
advance (other writers: the start in `CalculateFirstDeprAmount`, a reset in `TransferValues`, and `Done` in the
loop body). A mutant removing it could keep the loop alive. That is why the refusal matters, and it is in place.
The filing rested on R-604b's key search, which looked only at the R570 reach set, not at R487's scope. A key
search of that set alone cannot show that a procedure is unrefused.
