Shipped and measured live against Cronus283 at commit `4f5e896`. The tables gate moved from
killed 157 / survived 25 / no-coverage 10 over 192 deployed mutants (207 raw specs) to
killed 183 / survived 31 / no-coverage 10 over 224 deployed mutants (243 raw specs), score
183/214, untargetedTriggerCount 0. All 32 new mutants matched the verdicts pre-committed before
the run in `docs/superpowers/specs/2026-08-12-r134-filter-precommitment.md` (committed at
`c2390dd`), and the re-recorded baseline diff is purely additive, so no pre-existing mutant moved.

`lethal.flip-filter-literal` (Tier 2, 1.0.0) mutates INSIDE the filter string a `SetFilter` call
hands to Business Central, through a four-rule ladder: flip a `<>` comparator to `=`, shift a
`<`/`<=`/`>`/`>=` boundary to its paired counterpart, reverse an open range (`..X` to `X..`), or
drop a placeholder-free alternative from a `|` list. It is the first operator in the product that
mutates a string BC re-parses at RUNTIME rather than AL that `alc` compiles, and that distinction
drives most of what follows.

Seven fixture arms in `codeunit 79317 "Data Filter Ops"` measure it, each with its own `"Main No."`
tag and `"Entry No."` band so no arm can see another's rows:

- Arm A, rule 1, killed: `'<>%1'` to `'=%1'` counts the other group, 1 instead of 2.
- Arm B, rule 1, SURVIVED: the same rewrite under an existence-only assertion, which cannot see
  which group was counted.
- Arm C, rule 2, killed: `'<%1'` to `'<=%1'` with a row sitting exactly AT the threshold.
- Arm D, rule 2, SURVIVED: the same rewrite with a gap at the boundary, so the shifted comparator
  selects the identical row and the mutant is genuinely equivalent.
- Arm E, rule 3, killed: `'..%1'` to `'%1..'`, seeded so no row sits at the bound, which removes
  every verdict's dependence on the design's unmeasured range-inclusivity premise.
- Arm G, rule 4, killed: `'FLT-G-DECOY|%1'` to `'%1'`.
- Arm H, the refusal negative: a closed range classifies successfully and then exhausts the ladder,
  so the site emits nothing.

Arms C and D are the sharpest pair: identical rule, identical mutation shape, opposite verdicts,
decided purely by whether a row sits at the shifted boundary. Arms A and B are the same
construction for rule 1. A survivor cannot be manufactured by a platform error, which is why the
wave's claim rests on those two rather than on the four kills.

The four questions this row said a spec must settle, answered by what was built:

1. **Refuse-by-default parser.** Built. Arm H is its live negative, and it exercises LADDER
   EXHAUSTION on a shape that classifies fine, not the cheap character refusal. The character-class
   refusals (wildcards, case-insensitive markers, the AND combinator, inner quoting) are covered by
   unit tests only and have no live negative anywhere. Filed as R141 rather than assumed.
2. **`%N` substitution integrity.** Rule 4 drops only PLACEHOLDER-FREE alternatives, so the call's
   own argument list is never touched and arity cannot drift. Arm G is the live demonstration:
   `FLT-G-DECOY` was never backed by a call argument, so removing it needs no other edit.
3. **Identity and dedup.** This row's own assumption was wrong and the correction is the interesting
   part: the mutated node is the whole CALL, not the literal. That is what lets the mutant coexist
   with Tier-1 `void-method-call`, whose `before` is also the call but whose replacement text is
   empty, so the two identities always differ by that one field. Confirmed live at all six spans:
   each carries both mutants, and none of this operator's specs is displaced. The four new dedup
   displacements in this wave are the ordinary `remove-setrange` ones at arms C, D, E and H.
4. **Fixture arms with pre-committed verdicts.** 32 predictions, 32 matches.

**A matching verdict on a kill does not by itself prove the suite caught it**, because a mutated
filter is data BC re-parses at runtime and a platform rejection scores `killed` with nothing
recording why (R86, R138). The pre-commitment flagged that and named the settling evidence in
advance. The stored `killingTestFailure` settles it in the strong direction: each of the four kills
carries the arm's own bare `Error(...)` text naming the exact predicted count, and no BC
filter-evaluation message appears anywhere in the wave.

    arm A rule 1  expected 2 rows other than FILT-A1, got 1
    arm C rule 2  expected 2 rows below the threshold entry, got 3
    arm E rule 3  expected 2 entries at or below the bound, got 1
    arm G rule 4  expected the decoy and target rows together (5), got 3

Two collateral findings, both recorded rather than absorbed. The design's collateral list omitted
`lethal.conditional-boundary`, which fires at arm B's `exit(Related.Count() > 0)` and rewrites it to
`>= 0`, a tautology on a row count; the census found it before the run, its verdict was predicted as
a survivor, and the design's section 3.3 now names it. Arm D's hash decoy had to deviate from the
spec's literal text: `'FLT-NONE'` is valid on a `Code[20]` field but raises on an Integer one, which
a probe against Cronus283 measured directly, so the decoy there is a numeric out-of-band sentinel.

`assertFilterLiteralEvidence` in `packages/runner/itest/tables.itest.ts` pins what no baseline row
can express: six flip mutants and only six, none at arm H, each arm's before and after literal text,
and the verdict of the `void-method-call` sharing each span. Two of those spans disagree in opposite
directions (arm D's flip survives while the deletion is killed, arm G's flip is killed while the
deletion survives). A baseline row carries no site text and no pairing, and the baseline is
re-recorded by the run that first measures a growth, so it cannot catch anything on that run.

What this does NOT establish. Nothing about a rate on real code: the population census (288
`SetFilter` calls in a 658-file snapshot, 95 carrying `<>`, about 134 a comparator, 43 a `|` list,
14 a `..` range) counts syntax, not killable sites. Rule 1's real reach is materially below its raw
count, because the commonest `<>` idiom in real AL is `<>''`, which the parser refuses on the
embedded quote before the ladder runs, and this wave does not measure how far below. Arms B and D
each demonstrate ONE deliberate equivalent survivor and neither measures how often that class
occurs. Arm H proves the closed-range REFUSAL holds and says nothing about whether closed ranges
are worth mutating some other way later.

The refusal this row already recorded stands: **`remove-setfilter` is refused**, because
`void-method-call` already emits the identical deletion at every such site (same reasoning that
closed R11/R13).
