Coord task R-972, from preproc's R-969 (Contoso Coffee Demo Dataset peaked at 18.3 GB, `/coord/handoff/R-969/contosocoffeed.rss.tsv`).
Related: [[R948]] (the daemon recycle bound), [[R953]] / [[R972]] (admission by memory).

**Which process, and why R948 did not catch it.** Re-run with every process of the tree sampled every 2 s
(`/coord/handoff/R-972/measure.sh`, `base.procs.tsv`; edge-c8fe56e05, the same source, tests and config): LethAL's own process stayed
at 0.5 GB; the one al-runner serve daemon grew from 1.6 GB to 21.2 GB inside single prefetch requests of 256 entries (about 0.6 GB a
second) and fell back after each. R948's recycle reads the daemon's RSS between requests, after it fell, so it never fired.

**What drives it (measured).** 64 entries per request instead of 256 (`cap64.*`): daemon peak 6.3 GB, all 20,790 verdicts and killing
tests identical: about 77 MB an entry over a 1.35 GB floor. NOT the app's size: the same run restricted with `--only` to the 21 files
holding its covered mutants (604 deployed instead of 20,790, `only604.*`) peaked at 21.0 GB with the same requests. Invented apps do not
reproduce it (`repro/`: 20,000 procedures, or a test inserting 20,000 rows, stay under 330 MB at 256 entries), so it rests on what
Contoso's demo-data tests do, and no upstream issue is filed until an invented repro shows it.

**Built: measure, and start small.** While each serve request runs, the daemon's RSS is read every 200 ms; an entry costs (peak - RSS
at the start) / entries, and the session keeps the highest cost seen (never an average; it never falls; a reading of 0 or less adds
nothing). The next request's cap (`entriesChunkCap`, `al-runner-v3-entries.ts`) is a slow start: 16 entries before any reading, then at
most twice the largest measured request, and at most what fits 4 GiB at the cost seen; 1 to 256. The baseline runs one-shot, so there is
no reading before the first prefetch: hence the probe. The prefetch, R912's batch-refusal chunk placement and R778's kill-pair batches cut
each chunk when it is sent; a lowered cap is announced once (`al-runner-v3-entries-cap-lowered`). No RSS reader (no `/proc`): the
requests stay at 16. Tests inject the readings: a high one caps later requests (16, 16, 8 over 40 entries), a low one grows them
(16, 24).

**Measured on the build** (`slow.*`, the same run): cost 53 MiB an entry, cap 77; daemon peak 7.2 GB, whole tree 7.66 GB (under
`--peak serve`, 8 GiB = 8.59 GB); all 20,790 verdicts and killing tests equal to the 256-entry run; session 79 s instead of 220 s.

**Not changed.** Admission's `--peak serve` stays 8, and R948's between-request bound. A single mutant group larger than the cap still
goes alone (one echo, every covering codeunit): a widely covered mutant can still make one large request.
