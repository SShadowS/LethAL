# PR-460 pre-commitment (R826): a method-size ladder on invented AL, then a default for very large methods

Coord PR-460 (lethal-preproc, 2026-11-26). Written before any server call. It narrows R826's draft-2 plan (§"Measurement
plan") to the question DOTests raised. Evidence lives in `/coord/handoff/PR-460/`.

## Why now (step 1, `step1-warning.md`)
- **The hangs:** DOTests' Queue hung bc4 twice (lethal-bugs' PR-427). Both runs carried R-875's `large-method` warning for
  `CDOEMailJob.Table.al`: one method of 2,612 KB, chain 161.
  - The warning says `--max-guards-per-batch` "will not help: it splits by file".
  - Leaving out every listed operator together still leaves 1,986 KB.
- **How each run ended:** `in-flight-unknown` during the MUTANT phase ("RunMutant timed out before headers ... BC never
  answered"). The quarantine named that method as the unproven #28 suspect.
- **What is not known:** whether a ~2.6 MB method hangs or crashes a BC tier at all. #28 was a stack overflow in a BASELINE on
  BC 28 Windows. The only size measured here was 23.9 MB on BC 29 Linux, whose publish was refused (OutOfMemory) with the
  tier up.

## The ladder (one ladder, by emitted method size S)
- **The AL (invented, no corpus text):**
  - a target table and one codeunit whose ONE procedure holds K repeated straight-line blocks (assignments, `if`s, calls on
    the record);
  - **NO loop:** a loop makes hang-capable sites, which R164 keeps out of every gate but `itest:hang`, and R826's plan keeps
    loops out. The one-chain fold comes from `empty-block` on the procedure body instead, which joins the whole procedure
    into one chain (R827's measured lead shape);
  - one test codeunit with one test that calls the procedure once and asserts a field.
- **The rungs by S,** read from LethAL's own dry-run `large-method` figure on the real generator, all Tier-1 operators
  (there are no loops for loop operators to touch; no Tier-2): **0.5, 1, 2.6, 5 and 10 MB**.
  - K is chosen per rung offline, so each rung's S, chain, guards and operators are known before the server sees it.
  - Each rung's app must compile with alc offline.
- **Server:** one of bc1-bc3 (BC 28.4 W1, Linux), under a coord lease held for the whole ladder, never bc4 while lethal-bugs
  runs DOTests.
  - **The probe apps:** their own app ids and an object id range clear of LethAL Control (91000-91099) and the fixtures.
    They are unpublished after the ladder, tests app first. The fixtures are not touched.
- **Per rung, one `lethal run` (bcdev):**
  - a fresh `--db`, `--workers 1`, `--allow-large-run`;
  - `--baseline-timeout-ms 600000` (a cold start on a multi-MB method can look like a hang);
  - `--time-budget 900`, so the mutant phase runs about 15 minutes of mutants inside the method, where DOTests hung;
  - `--stop-hung-sessions`;
  - then `lethal doctor`, plus the control app and the target read back as installed (an auto-restart wipes the in-memory
    database).
- **Reading a rung:**
  - **pass:** publish OK, the baseline green, mutants scored with no `in-flight-unknown`, the doctor clean;
  - **refused at publish:** recorded as that (as at 23.9 MB);
  - **hang or crash:** an `in-flight-unknown`, a quarantine, or the tier not answering (read twice, a minute apart);
  - **unknown:** anything else.
- **The order and the stop.** Rungs climb from 0.5 MB.
  - **The first hang or crash stops the ladder:** no retry and no further call. The lease stays held, and the restart is
    asked of kraken/the owner (`kraken coord ask --kind bc-restart`). R826's restart budget (5) is counted.
  - A publish refusal does not stop the ladder below it, but rungs above it are skipped.
- **Recorded per rung:** S, chain, guards deployed, operators, the outcome, load at the start and end, exit, logs, the
  doctor.

## The default, decided by the ladder (pre-committed rule)
Let F be the smallest rung that hangs, crashes or is refused, and P the largest rung that passed below it.
- **If F exists and F ≤ 10 MB:** ship an **auto-split**.
  - When a file's largest emitted method is over P, its mutants are split across batches so that no batch emits a method
    over P. A batch instruments only its subset, so the fold shrinks guard by guard (PR-67's slices measured about 45 KB
    per guard).
  - If one subset cannot get under P (a single mutant's fold over P), that file is **refused by name** before any publish,
    with the figures and the operator levers.
  - The `large-method` warning cites the measured P and F.
- **If no rung up to 10 MB fails:** no limit ships. The warning keeps its wording, now citing "passed up to 10 MB on BC 28.4
  Linux".
  - DOTests' hang is then not explained by size, and is filed as its own item.
- **The default holds for the build and platform measured:** the warning says so, and does not present a Linux figure as safe
  for Windows (R826).
- **The build of the default** (auto-split or refusal) gets its own pre-commitment, with red tests, after the ladder.

## Amendment 1 (2026-11-26, from the offline build, before any server call): two families, and where the fold comes from
- **Correction:** "`empty-block` on the procedure body joins the whole procedure into one chain" is WRONG on today's code.
  - Since R827, `empty-block` on a body is a free guard: it wraps the body once, copies nothing and starts no chain.
  - Only loops and statements holding a `#if` still collect everything inside into one chain (`isFlat`, `isFreeShape` in
    `packages/schemata/src/components.ts`).
  - The offline build measured it: removing `empty-block` changes S by under 0.5%.
- **So the ladder has two families,** built and sized by LethAL's real generator, and compiled clean with alc (app, tests
  and the instrumented build). Evidence: `/coord/handoff/PR-460/ladder/rungs.md`.
  - **chain (rungs 5-9) is DOTests' shape:** one long chain, from a statement holding an empty `#if` region whose symbol is
    never defined (no run-time effect). There is no loop, so no hang-capable mutant.

    | rung | S (KB) | chain | guards |

    | 6 | 1,034 | 117 | 117 |
    | 7 | **2,607** | **173** | 173 |
    | 8 | 4,936 | 229 | 229 |
    | 9 | 10,040 | 341 | 341 |

    Rung 7 is next to DOTests' 2,612 KB at chain 161.
  - **flat (rungs 0-4) tests S alone:** many short chains (chain 5).

    | rung | S (KB) | guards |
    |---|---|---|
    | 0 | 513 | 2,471 |
    | 1 | 999 | 4,811 |

- **The order:**
  - **The chain family first** (it is the DOTests question), climbing 5 -> 9, and stopping at the first hang or crash as
    pre-committed.
  - **The flat family second,** only if the chain family has no hang or crash below rung 9, and only within the owner's
    restart budget. It separates S from the chain.
  - The default rule reads P and F from the chain family. When the flat family fails at a size where the chain family
    passed, the limit is on S alone, and the rule uses the smaller P.
- **Per run:** `--selector-id`, `--control-id` and `--table-id` inside each rung's own id range (64600 + 20·slot; the CLI
  defaults sit inside sandbox-app's range).
- **Not predicted offline:** a clean offline alc compile does not run the server's object-emit step, which is where BC 29
  refused the 23.9 MB method. Only the server answers that.

## Amendment 2 (2026-11-26, after the ladder ran on bc2): the default is on GUARDS per method call, not bytes
- **What the ladder measured** (`/coord/handoff/PR-460/runs/RESULT.md`; bc2, BC 28.4 W1 Linux, one owner-approved restart):
  - **Chain family (DOTests' shape), ALL PASS to 10 MB:** 515 KB/89 guards up to 10,040 KB/341 guards. Every mutant was
    scored, and the baseline call took 1-23 s. Bytes and chain length are NOT the limit on 28.4. **So DOTests' bc4 hang (BC
    29) is not explained by method size on BC 28.4.** Whether BC 29 differs is not measured.
  - **Flat family (many short chains):**
    - 2,471 guards: the single baseline call took 60 s, pass;
    - 4,811 guards: 91 s, pass;
    - **12,507 guards: over 300 s.** The call was cut at the gateway's 300 s request ceiling, the run was quarantined, and
      the operation stayed until an NST restart. **This is F.**
  - **The cost of one call grows with the guards EXECUTED in it,** and faster than linearly. A straight line through the
    two passes predicts 192 s at 12.5k, yet more than 300 s was measured.
- **The pre-committed rule (replaces the byte rule above):**
  - **The metric:** G, the guards deployed in the file's largest emitted method. When a test runs that method once, every
    component executes, so G bounds the guard checks per call. This comes from the real generator, offline, before any
    publish.
  - **The threshold:** `MAX_GUARDS_PER_METHOD = 4,800`. That is the last measured pass (4,811 guards, 91 s, about 30% of
    the 300 s ceiling): at or under the last passing rung, as R826 requires, with no extrapolation above it.
  - **Above it, LethAL SPLITS:**
    - the file's mutants go across batches, so that no batch emits a method over 4,800 guards;
    - a batch instruments only its subset, so G falls guard by guard;
    - the split runs before any publish, and its notice names the file, the method, G and the number of batches.
    - Because every guard is one mutant, a split under the threshold always exists. **A refusal by name** happens only if
      one mutant's own fold were over 4,800 guards, which no shape here produces. The code keeps it as a guard, never as
      the plan.
  - **The `large-method` byte warning stays a WARNING.** Its text now cites the ladder: up to 10 MB passed in one long
    chain on BC 28.4 Linux, and the limit measured is guards per call, not bytes.
  - **The platform:** the threshold holds for BC 28.4 W1 Linux, behind a 300 s request ceiling, as measured. The notice
    says so, and it is not presented as safe for Windows or BC 29 (R826).
- **The build** gets red tests both ways:
  - a 4,801-guard method splits, and a 4,800 one does not;
  - each batch's largest method is ≤ 4,800;
  - verdicts are equal to the unsplit run on a fixture where both fit;
  - the refusal fires only for a single fold over the threshold.
  - Then VERIFY, the gates per legs.ts, and CI.

## Amendment 3 (2026-11-26, the owner's caveat after the ladder)
- **`MAX_GUARDS_PER_METHOD = 4,800` is a BC 28.4-measured threshold.** The ~12.5k-guards-per-call failure was seen on BC 28.4 W1
  Linux only. Until it is seen on BC 29, it is treated as BC 28.4's (owner, 2026-11-26). DOTests' hangs were on `bc4`, BC 29.
- **So the build names it that way:** the constant's comment, the split notice and the `large-method` warning say "measured on
  BC 28.4 W1 Linux", and none presents it as a BC 29 or Windows limit.
- **Recovery after F, for the record:** one owner restart of bc2, then setup, `force-reset-lease` and `clear-quarantine` under the
  held lease; the four fixture doctors were clean, the probe apps were gone (read by app id), and the lease was released.

## Amendment 4 (2026-11-26, coord PR-461, before any code): a REFUSAL by name, not a split
- **Ruling (lethal-orchestrator, 2026-11-26; principles 13, 5, 4):** refuse by name instead of splitting. The split fires on
  no known project (the moves below), and the spec-adversary found it needs 7 fixes that only a v3 split test could prove
  (`/coord/handoff/PR-461/adversary-r1.md`). The split is filed as R1231, with those fixes as its checklist, for the first
  real project that hits this refusal. This replaces amendment 2's "Above it, LethAL SPLITS".
- **The rule:** on bcdev only, before any publish (in `runSession`, before `planArtifacts`), a file whose largest method
  holds more than `MAX_GUARDS_PER_METHOD = 4,800` guards refuses the run. The dry run says the same as a NOTE where it
  already prints the `large-method` note (bcdev, or no backend named). al-runner v3 does not refuse: it is a BC tier's limit.
- **G, from the generator's plan, not from text:** the file's deduped specs, ids and `buildComponents`, as the batch's compile
  sees them. Each component's members are its guards; each component belongs to its nearest enclosing `procedure` or
  `trigger_declaration` (or a procedure-like split node). G is the largest per-method sum.
  - **Cheap first:** G is at most the file's deduped count, so a file at or under 4,800 is not planned at all.
  - **Loud on mismatch:** a component with no enclosing method, or per-method sums that do not add up to the deduped count,
    throws by name. Never a G of 0.
- **The refusal:** a typed error (extending `Error` directly), before any publish. It names the file, the method (its name
  and line), G, the threshold, "measured on BC 28.4 W1 Linux (R826): not measured on BC 29 or Windows", and the levers:
  narrow with `--lines` or `--only` until that method is under 4,800 guards, or leave operators out with
  `--exclude-operator`. The constant's comment says the same.
- **The `large-method` warning** still fires on bytes. Its last sentence changes: it cites the ladder (one long chain passed
  to 10 MB on BC 28.4 Linux; the limit measured is guards per call) and says that a method over 4,800 guards is refused
  on bcdev, in place of "--max-guards-per-batch will not help: it splits by file".
- **Red tests, both ways, each red-checked:** G = threshold + 1 refuses and G = threshold runs (the threshold is a parameter
  of the check, so a test sets it low); many guards over small methods (file count over, G under) runs; the refusal names
  the file, method, G and levers; a mismatch throws; v3 does not refuse; the dry run prints the note.
- **The moves, measured (`/coord/handoff/PR-461/moves/`, the real generator, default operators, whole projects):**
  no gate fixture and no DO project has a file over 4,800 guards, so no method there can be (largest file: fixtures 125,
  DO Cloud 1,580, DO Test 1,281). In DC, one Cloud file has 5,626 guards, and its largest method holds 557: it is not
  refused (the "many guards, small methods" case, real). DC Test's largest file is 3,564. **Expected moves: 0** on every
  gate, on every leg, warning sets included. Any move is a BLOCK.
