# PR-274: how the wall of a run scales with `--workers` (al-runner v3, a 22-file DO slice)

Source: coord PR-274, `/coord/handoff/PR-274/result.md`. This note keeps the findings the code now relies on. No DO source
text is in it: only names and numbers.

## What was run
- A 22-file DO slice: 3,597 mutants, 8 arms at 3, 4, 6 and 8 workers, interleaved, a fresh store per arm, `--profile slim`.
- LethAL `3a7996a5a`; al-runner `main-ccd0f823d`, pinned by path.
- The box was shared (the owner refused a quiet window). The memory gate was kept; the host-load gate was dropped.

## What it found
- **Verdicts are identical at every lane count.**
- **Mutant-phase wall:** W6a 2,811 s, W6b 2,828 s, W8a 2,547 s. Four lanes took 2,919 s. Past 4 lanes the wall is almost flat.
- **8 against 6:** W8a / W6 = 0.90, so only about 10% better.
- **The tail:** at 6 and 8 lanes most lanes sit idle for their last 400-1,060 s while one lane finishes. W8a's lanes were
  56-80% busy.
- **Memory:** no daemon passed 2.06 GiB in any arm. The whole tree reached 17.3 GiB at 8 lanes. CPU was not binding (8 lanes
  used 5.4 cores).

## The lane rule
lanes = min(8, floor((P - 4 GiB) / D)), where:
- D is the highest daemon peak of the session (2.06 GiB measured on DO);
- P is the smaller of what the run needs and what the container can give (`memory.max` minus the 4 GiB reserve minus what is
  used at admission);
- 4 GiB is the lane gate's own charge for LethAL.

The heavy-slot `--peak` to declare is 4 + n x D, rounded up: 13 for 4 lanes, 17 for 6, 21-22 for 8.

## The pre-committed peak rule was too generous
- The pre-commitment used 4 + 3n GiB (26 GiB for 8 lanes). It assumed 3 GiB per lane.
- Measured, a lane needs about 2.06 GiB. And 26 GiB never fits 8 lanes on server1: the container sits at about 2.5 GiB at rest,
  and the slot needs used + peak + 4 GiB reserve <= 32 GiB.
- Amendment 2 (`09d4cadcc`) set W8a's declared peak to 22 GiB from the measured daemon maximum.

## The per-scope premise (why PR-281 orders the scopes)
The floor past 4 lanes looked like one long chain. It is not one scope. Taken from PR-273's per-mutant model on perf10c,
restricted to this slice and grouped by procedure scope:
- 212 scopes, 8,408 lane-s at perf10c's scale (about 1.3-1.6x that at PR-274's speeds).
- The largest four are 913, 783, 635 and 634 lane-s (10.9%, 9.3%, 7.6%, 7.5%). Two of them sit late in manifest order: scopes
  #117 and #161 of 212.
- So several large scopes reach lanes late. At 8 lanes the wall should approach the larger of the biggest scope (about
  913 x 1.5 = 1,400 s) and the total / 8 (about 1,700 s). W8a took 2,547 s.

The queue handed out scopes strictly in manifest order. PR-281 hands out the costliest first, inside each window, and keeps
`LETHAL_V3_UNIT_ORDER` for the A/B. PR-281's costliest-first order is opt-in (`LETHAL_V3_UNIT_ORDER=costliest`), because its A/B was load-dominated: the prediction was missed, and the idle tail was 23% lower. A scope is never split across lanes, because that would change the R197 kill
ledger's order inside it.

## Limits of this note
- A full DO run is about 6x this slice, so the tail is a smaller share of it. That is an estimate, not a measurement.
- Slice-sized runs only: no claim is made for other apps.
